## Meaning

The line 1-5-9 is the rising diagonal of the [[birth-grid|birth grid]] in David A. Phillips’s layout, from the bottom left to the top right. It crosses all three planes: [[number-1|1]] for the body, [[number-5|5]] at the centre for the soul, [[number-9|9]] for the mind. It joins initiative, freedom and the wide, idealistic view: the first digit, the middle one and the last.

When all three cells hold a digit, the line is full. Phillips names it the arrow of determination and finds it the most common arrow of the 20th century. The tradition reads persistence: a goal, once chosen, is pursued to the end, and obstacles tend to sharpen the resolve. The gift asks for a goal worth the effort, and for patience with people who move at another pace.

When all three cells are empty, Phillips speaks of the arrow of procrastination. The empty diagonal cuts the grid in two, and the tradition reads a pull toward putting things off. Read kindly, it points to a person who may wait until a task feels clear before starting. Deadlines, small first steps and a friend to report to help. It is a habit to work with, not a judgement.

A partial line, with one or two of its digits, says little alone.

Every year from 1900 to 1999 holds a 1 and a 9. For a birth in those years, a single 5 in the day, the month or the year completes the line. Ulune’s code finds it full on about 33% of dates from 1900 to 1999, the most frequent arrow of that century. From 2000 on, it is full on about 5% of dates and empty on about 11%.

## In practice

Take a made-up birth date, 15 June 1990. Without the zeros its digits are 1 1 5 6 9 9. The diagonal holds two 1s, a 5 and two 9s: the line 1-5-9 is full, and Ulune lists it as “full: an arrow”.

Now take 26 August 2003. Its digits are 2 2 3 6 8. No 1, no 5, no 9: the diagonal is empty, and Ulune lists the line as “empty: what is missing”. The same date leaves the bottom row, [[grid-1-4-7|1-4-7]], empty too.

## Across traditions

The Lo Shu square is laid out 4 9 2 / 3 5 7 / 8 1 6. There, the digits 1, 5 and 9 form the centre column, with the 9 on top and the 1 below. Ulune lists it there as 9-5-1, as it is read on the square, and that name links to this page too. The line is the same in both layouts, only turned, and it is full or empty on the same dates. Its digits add up to 15, like every line of the Lo Shu.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune counts the digits of the local date of birth, day, month and year, with the zeros set aside. The line 1-5-9 is full when the date holds at least one 1, one 5 and one 9. It is empty when the date holds none of them, and partial otherwise, shown with a dash. A repeated digit counts once. The line is a diagonal of Phillips’s layout and the centre column of the Lo Shu, named 9-5-1 there. Its state is the same in both.
