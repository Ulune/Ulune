## Meaning

The Birthday number comes from the day of the month a person was born, from the 1st to the 31st. Numerology treats it as a secondary number. It points to one specific talent, a gift that is often visible early in life.

Where the [[life-path|Life Path]] describes the whole road, the Birthday is read as one tool carried along it. It is narrower and more concrete. A Birthday 4 is read as a talent for organisation and care with detail. A Birthday 3 as a knack for words, humour or music. A Birthday 8 as good judgement with money and people.

The tradition reads the Birthday as a help to the Life Path, not a second direction. When the two agree, the talent and the road pull the same way. When they differ, the Birthday is read as a resource for the Life Path’s lessons. A Life Path 7 with a Birthday 3, for instance, has a way of sharing what it studies.

The Birthday keeps two master numbers, 11 and 22. A 33 cannot occur, since no month has a 33rd day and no day’s digits add up to 33.

## In practice

The number comes straight from the day. Ulune reduces it by adding its digits:

- a made-up person born on the 3rd has a Birthday 3, which needs no reduction;
- Yolanda Mary Kyle, born on 29 November 1984, adds 2 + 9 = 11 and stops there: a Birthday 11, a talent for intuition and for sensing what goes unsaid;
- Hugo Pierre Lambert, born on 22 March 1987, keeps 22 whole: a talent for practical vision;
- Jean-Pierre Noël Fabre, born on 19 August 1962, adds 1 + 9 = 10, then 1 + 0 = 1.

Jean-Pierre’s Birthday is written 19/1. The 19 is a [[karmic-debt|karmic debt]], read as a lesson about independence. It asks for learning to accept help, and to use one’s strength for more than oneself. The tradition offers it as a theme to work with, never as a fault.

People born on the same day of any month share the Birthday number. The 1st, 10th, 19th and 28th all give 1, for example, though only the 19th carries a karmic debt.

## Across traditions

- **Indian numerology**, as practised today, gives the day of birth a number of its own, the *mulank*, often translated birth number. Its digits are added down to a single digit, so the 29th gives 2 there, not 11.
- **The Life Path** of the same tradition uses the day again, as one of the three parts of the date. The Birthday is that part read alone.
- **Astrology** has no equivalent of the day of the month. Its own calendar is the Sun’s place in the [[sign|signs]], which a day of the month does not give alone.

## Origin and history

Twentieth-century numerology built its chart from the date and the name. L. Dow Balliett, in 1908, adds the month, the day and the year together into a single birth number. Modern manuals also read the day alone, as the Birthday number, and tie it to one talent. Like the other core numbers, it belongs to this modern layer, not to ancient number lore.

Keeping 11 and 22 whole for the 11th, the 22nd and the 29th follows the master-number rule. Balliett already set 11 and 22 apart as “free” numbers. Hans Decoz, whose rules Ulune follows, calls the Birthday number a special talent, a gift. He also reads the day whole: the 15th and the 24th both reduce to 6, but read a little differently. Ulune keeps to the reduced number.

## How Ulune calculates it

- Ulune reads the local day of birth, as on the birth certificate, not the day in Universal Time.
- The day is reduced by adding its digits until one digit is left, or until 11 or 22 appears. So the 11th and the 29th give 11, the 22nd gives 22. 33 is never kept here.
- A day of 13, 14, 16 or 19 marks a karmic debt: 13/4, 14/5, 16/7 and 19/1.
- No name is needed: the Birthday is there with any birth date.
- The same day is also the second [[period-cycle|period cycle]], the middle stretch of life, and a part of the first two [[pinnacle|pinnacles]].
