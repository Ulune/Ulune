## Meaning

The Personality number comes from the consonants of the full name at birth. Numerology reads it as the impression a person makes on people who do not know them yet. It is what others notice first, before they see the deeper motives.

Consonants are the firm edges that give a name its shape. Numerology reads their number as the outer frame of a person: manner, style, the way they come across in a room. One way to picture it is as a door. Some people are let in through it easily, others wait until trust grows.

A Personality 4 is read as someone who looks reliable and practical at first sight. A Personality 3 comes across as lively and easy to talk to. A Personality 8 gives an impression of authority and competence.

The Personality is not a mask in a bad sense. It is the part of the self that meets the world first, and numerology often reads it as a shelter for the more private [[soul-urge|Soul Urge]]. Numerology reads the distance between the two, the [[bridge|bridge]], as the work of letting outer manner and inner wish agree.

## In practice

Take a made-up person, Bernard Olivier Martin. Ulune adds the consonants of each name:

- Bernard: B 2 + R 9 + N 5 + R 9 + D 4 = 29, then 11, kept as a master number;
- Olivier: L 3 + V 4 + R 9 = 16, then 7;
- Martin: M 4 + R 9 + T 2 + N 5 = 20, then 2.

The names together give 11 + 7 + 2 = 20, then 2. Bernard has a Personality 2. Numerology reads a first impression of tact and gentleness: someone who listens, notices the mood and seems easy to work with.

His vowels tell another story. They give a Soul Urge 9, a wide, idealistic inner wish. The bridge between 9 and 2 is 7. The tradition would suggest reflection and time alone as what helps the two meet.

## Across traditions

- **Astrology** has its own first impression: the [[ascendant|Ascendant]], the sign rising at birth, is read as the manner and the face shown to the world. Ulune offers this as a comparison between two languages, not as a rule. The two are worked out in entirely different ways.
- **Hebrew gematria** counts consonants only, because the script is written mostly without vowels. Its sums are not split into an inner and an outer number, though.
- **The [[chaldean|Chaldean number]]** reads the name as one total. It has no consonant number of its own.

## Origin and history

Older letter arithmetic, such as the method Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa describes in 1533, added a whole name at once. The split of a name into vowels and consonants belongs to 20th-century numerology. In 1908, L. Dow Balliett already reads the vowels as the hidden side of a name. Later manuals made the consonant total the third name number, read as the visible side. Ulune uses its most common name, Personality. French books often call it the *nombre de réalisation*.

Ulune follows Hans Decoz’s rules: each name reduced on its own, master numbers kept, and Y read by its place.

## How Ulune calculates it

- **Which name.** The full name at birth, typed for numerology. Without it, the chart’s own name if it is a typed one, with a note. Without any name, the Personality waits and shows a dash.
- **Consonants.** Every letter except A, E, I, O, U and a Y that sounds like a vowel. W is always a consonant. A Y is a consonant where it goes with a vowel, as in Yolanda, Taylor or Mickey. Each Y can be switched by hand.
- **Letter values.** J S = 1, B K T = 2, C L = 3, D M V = 4, N W = 5, F X = 6, G P Y = 7, H Q Z = 8, R = 9. Accents are dropped.
- **Reduction.** The consonants of each name are added and reduced, keeping 11, 22 and 33. The names are added and reduced again, keeping them too.
- **Karmic debt.** A 13, 14, 16 or 19 in the total’s steps marks a [[karmic-debt|karmic debt]]. Inside one name of several, such as the 16 in Olivier above, it does not.
- **A name used now**, such as a married name, gives its own minor Personality. It adds to the birth name’s, never replaces it.
