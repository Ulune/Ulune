## Meaning

The Expression number comes from every letter of the full name at birth. Many books call it the Destiny number. Numerology reads it as a person’s natural abilities and the way they tend to use them: how they work, speak and get things done.

Where the [[life-path|Life Path]] describes the road, the Expression describes the traveller’s equipment. It is read as potential rather than as a finished fact. An Expression 1 points to a talent for leading and originating. An Expression 2 points to tact and partnership, an Expression 3 to words, images and ideas.

The Expression holds the two other name numbers. Its vowels give the [[soul-urge|Soul Urge]], the inner motive. Its consonants give the [[personality-number|Personality]], the first impression. The tradition reads the Expression as both at work together, the whole person meeting the world.

Because it comes from the birth name, it does not change with a new name. A married name or a chosen name adds its own minor numbers, which colour the Expression without replacing it.

## In practice

Take a made-up person, Hugo Pierre Lambert. Ulune adds each name on its own:

- Hugo: H 8 + U 3 + G 7 + O 6 = 24, then 6;
- Pierre: P 7 + I 9 + E 5 + R 9 + R 9 + E 5 = 44, then 8;
- Lambert: L 3 + A 1 + M 4 + B 2 + E 5 + R 9 + T 2 = 26, then 8.

The names are then added: 6 + 8 + 8 = 22. The total is a master number, kept whole. Hugo has an Expression 22, the master builder. Numerology reads in it the method of the 4 on a larger scale, a talent for turning a big idea into something lasting. The tradition adds that a 22 is often lived as a 4 for years first.

The order of the steps matters. Adding all the letters at once gives 94, then 13, then 4: no master number, and a 13 instead. Hans Decoz’s World Numerology warns of exactly this: one long sum can create false master numbers or hide true ones. Ulune reduces each name first.

## Across traditions

- **The [[chaldean|Chaldean number]]** reads the same full name with Cheiro’s values, where letters run from 1 to 8 by sound. For Hugo Pierre Lambert, the letters total 65. Cheiro reads totals up to 52 as compound numbers, so 65 is reduced to 11, then to the single number 2. The two systems give different numbers: they are separate languages.
- **Indian numerology** today calls its name number the *namank*, worked out from the letters of the name as a whole.
- **Gematria** in Hebrew and **isopsephy** in Greek add a word’s letters too, but to compare words of equal value, not to read a character.

## Origin and history

Adding a name’s letters to learn about its bearer is old. Agrippa, in 1533, reports that the Pythagoreans gave letters “divine numbers”. He describes adding the numbers of a name and dividing by nine, then reading what remains. Modern numerology’s Pythagorean label is a later attribution of the same kind: nothing Pythagoras is known to have written describes it.

The Expression as we know it belongs to 20th-century numerology. In 1908, L. Dow Balliett adds the letters of the full name to find its number. Later manuals named the whole-name number Expression or Destiny, and set it beside the vowel and consonant numbers. The two names still live side by side.

## How Ulune calculates it

- **Which name.** The full name at birth, typed for numerology: every first name, middle name and surname. Without it, Ulune uses the chart’s own name when it is a typed one, and says so. Without any name, the Expression shows a dash and waits.
- **Letter values.** A J S = 1, B K T = 2, C L U = 3, D M V = 4, E N W = 5, F O X = 6, G P Y = 7, H Q Z = 8, I R = 9. Accents are dropped (É is E); Œ counts as O and E, and ß as two S.
- **Names.** The name is split at its spaces. A hyphenated name such as Jean-Pierre is one name.
- **Reduction.** Each name is added and reduced, keeping 11, 22 and 33. The results are added and reduced again, keeping them too.
- **Karmic debt.** A 13, 14, 16 or 19 in the total’s steps marks a [[karmic-debt|karmic debt]]. With a one-word name, its own steps count. A 13 inside a single name of several does not.
- Every letter counts, so the Y’s role as vowel or consonant changes nothing here.
