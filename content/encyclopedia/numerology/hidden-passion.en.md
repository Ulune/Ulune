## Meaning

Every letter of a name falls on a number from 1 to 9. The hidden passion is the number the most letters fall on. Numerology reads it as a drive or a talent a person tends to reach for without thinking. It is a strength that keeps showing up because the name holds so much of it.

It is hidden in a simple sense: it is not one of the core numbers, and it never appears as a total. It shows only when the letters are counted one by one, and it can quietly colour everything else. A hidden passion 5 is read as a love of freedom and experience: curiosity and adaptability come easily, and so can restlessness. A hidden passion 6 is read as a love of care, home and family: responsibility comes naturally, and so can taking on too much.

The tradition reads it in two directions. It is a gift, a talent that grows almost by itself. It is also a pull: so much of one quality can crowd out the others. The texts Ulune uses always name both sides.

The hidden passion has a mirror. The numbers no letter falls on are the [[karmic-lesson|karmic lessons]], qualities that may have to be learned on purpose. A name rich in one number and missing another is read as a person with a strong lean and a quiet corner.

## In practice

Take a made-up person, Bernard Olivier Martin. Ulune gives each of his 20 letters its value and counts them:

| Number | Letters | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A, A | 2 |
| 2 | B, T | 2 |
| 3 | L | 1 |
| 4 | D, V, M | 3 |
| 5 | E, N, E, N | 4 |
| 6 | O | 1 |
| 7 | none | 0 |
| 8 | none | 0 |
| 9 | R, R, I, I, R, R, I | 7 |

Seven letters fall on 9, far more than any other number. Bernard has a hidden passion 9: a passion for ideals and for people everywhere. Generosity comes naturally; so can forgetting his own needs. His karmic lessons are 7 and 8, the two numbers with no letter.

Ties are common. Yolanda Mary Kyle, another made-up name, has three letters on 1 and three on 7. Ulune reads both: a passion for leading and being her own person, and one for knowledge and understanding.

## Across traditions

- **The [[birth-grid|birth grid]]** does with the digits of the date what the hidden passion does with the letters of the name. It places each digit on a square of nine. A repeated digit stands out at a glance, and a full line of three is read as a strength.
- **Astrology** has a similar idea of weight. A [[stellium|stellium]], several planets gathered in one sign or house, is read as a strong focus there. Setting the two side by side is a comparison for learning, not a rule of either tradition.
- **The [[chaldean|Chaldean number]]** does not count letters by value. It reads the name’s total only.

## Origin and history

Letter arithmetic is old. In 1533, Agrippa reports the Pythagorean belief that letters carry numbers, and he adds up whole names. Counting how often each number appears among the letters is a later idea. It belongs to modern numerology, together with the karmic lessons, the planes of expression and the other finer numbers. No ancient source describes it, and its Pythagorean label is a later attribution.

Ulune follows Hans Decoz’s school for the rules. It counts all the letters of the full birth name, and it reads every tie.

## How Ulune calculates it

- **Which name.** The full name at birth, typed for numerology, or the chart’s typed name when no birth name is given. A name used now does not change it. Without any name, there is no hidden passion.
- **Letter values.** The Pythagorean table: A J S = 1, B K T = 2, C L U = 3, D M V = 4, E N W = 5, F O X = 6, G P Y = 7, H Q Z = 8, I R = 9. Accents are dropped.
- **Counting.** Every letter counts once, vowels and consonants alike. A Y counts on 7 whether it is read as a vowel or a consonant.
- **The result.** The number or numbers with the highest count. When several numbers tie, Ulune reads them all, and shows the count: “7 letters, the most”.
- Nothing is added or reduced, so there are no master numbers and no karmic debts here.
