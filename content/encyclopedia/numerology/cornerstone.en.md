## Meaning

The cornerstone is the first letter of the first name. In a building, the cornerstone is the first stone laid, the one the walls rise from. Numerology borrows the image: the first letter of the name a person is called by sets the tone of how they meet things.

Ulune reads it as how a person approaches what comes, opportunities and obstacles alike. It is the first gesture: whether someone steps forward, waits and observes, plans, or reaches out to others.

Unlike most positions, the cornerstone is read as a letter, not only as a number. Each letter has a character of its own, and also carries its [[pythagorean|Pythagorean]] value. A and S are both 1, yet Ulune reads them a little differently: A is ambition and independence, S is charm and drive.

The cornerstone is one of three special letters of the first name, the [[stones|stones]]. The [[capstone|capstone]], the last letter, describes how things are finished. The [[first-vowel|first vowel]] gives a glimpse of the inner self.

The same letter is also the first initial of the [[balance-number|balance number]], and it opens the physical [[letter-cycle|letter cycle]]. That cycle walks through the first name from birth, each letter lasting its value in years.

## In practice

Take a made-up person, Bernard Olivier Martin, born 3 November 1978. His first name is Bernard, so his cornerstone is B, worth 2.

Ulune’s reading for B: sensitivity and warmth, attentive to others and generous, sometimes shy or easily hurt. As a cornerstone, numerology reads this as the colour of his first step: a new situation approached through people, the mood sensed before acting.

His value 2 links the cornerstone to the [[number-2|2]], the number of cooperation. In his physical letter cycle, B covers his first two years of life, then E takes over for five.

## Origin and history

The cornerstone belongs to modern name numerology. Matthew Oliver Goodwin’s guide (1981) already gives a chapter to the first letter and the first vowel, read as a person’s approach to experience. Hans Decoz, whose rules Ulune follows, lists the cornerstone among the numbers of a complete chart, in his book with Tom Monte (1994).

## How Ulune calculates it

- Ulune takes the full name at birth, as typed for numerology, or else the chart’s own typed name.
- The first name is the first word, up to the first space. A hyphenated first name counts whole: Jean-Pierre gives J.
- Accents are dropped, so Élodie gives E. The cornerstone is that first letter, shown with its Pythagorean value.
- A Y is the cornerstone whatever its role: in Yolanda it is read as a consonant.
- A one-letter first name is its own cornerstone and capstone, read once. Without a name, there is no cornerstone.
