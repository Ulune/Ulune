## Meaning

The capstone is the last letter of the first name. In building, a capstone is the stone set on top to finish a wall or an arch. Numerology borrows the image: the letter that closes the first name says something about how a person closes things.

Ulune reads it as how a person brings things to an end: finishing a task, completing a project, and letting go of what is over. It is the last gesture of an undertaking, where the [[cornerstone|cornerstone]] is the first.

Some people start well and lose interest before the end; others hesitate to begin, then see everything through. Read beside the cornerstone, the capstone shows whether the first and last steps share a tone.

Like the cornerstone, the capstone is read as a letter with a character of its own, and through its [[pythagorean|Pythagorean]] value. A capstone E, worth 5, brings freedom and communication to the finish: curious, lively, already looking at what comes next.

The capstone is one of the three [[stones|stones]] of the first name. The third is the [[first-vowel|first vowel]], a glimpse of the inner self.

## In practice

Take a made-up person, Bernard Olivier Martin, born 3 November 1978. His first name, Bernard, begins with B and ends with D. His capstone is D, worth 4.

Ulune’s reading for D: order and endurance, practical, hard-working and reliable, sometimes stubborn. As a capstone, numerology reads this as a careful finish. Things are brought to a solid end, step by step, rather than dropped once the interest fades.

His cornerstone B, worth 2, is gentle and attentive to others. The pair suggests someone who starts through people and finishes through method. His value 4 links the capstone to the [[number-4|4]], the number of work and structure.

The capstone also closes each round of the physical [[letter-cycle|letter cycle]], which walks through the first name from birth. For Bernard, D covers the ages of 31 to 35, before the cycle starts again with B.

## Origin and history

The capstone belongs to modern name numerology. Ulune follows Hans Decoz, who lists it among the numbers of a complete chart, in his book with Tom Monte (1994).

## How Ulune calculates it

- Ulune takes the full name at birth, as typed for numerology, or else the chart’s own typed name.
- The first name is the first word, up to the first space. A hyphenated first name counts whole: Jean-Pierre ends with E.
- Accents are dropped, and signs that are not letters, such as an apostrophe, are left aside.
- The capstone is the last letter, shown with its Pythagorean value. A Y counts whatever its role, vowel in Mary, consonant in Mickey.
- In a one-letter first name, such as an initial, the cornerstone and the capstone are one letter, read once.
