## Meaning

The line 3-6-9 is the top row of the [[birth-grid|birth grid]] as David A. Phillips lays it out. He calls this row the plane of the mind: the head of the grid, where numerology reads thinking, memory, imagination and ideals. It gathers [[number-3|3]], expression and creativity, [[number-6|6]], care and responsibility, and [[number-9|9]], the wide and generous view.

The three digits are a family. Every multiple of 3 reduces to 3, 6 or 9, so the row holds the digits of the whole three-times table.

When all three cells hold a digit, the line is full. Phillips names it the arrow of the intellect. The tradition reads a mind that is busy and alert: it analyses, compares and remembers, and notices quickly when something does not add up. The gift asks for balance with feeling and the body, the two rows below, so that thinking does not run alone.

When all three cells are empty, Phillips speaks of the arrow of poor memory. Read kindly, it describes a mind that grows by use. Notes, routines and calm repetition help, and so does learning through the hands and the heart. Numerology sees it as a field to train, not as a verdict.

A line with one or two of its digits is partial. It says little alone; the digits present keep their own meaning.

Every year from 1900 to 1999 holds a 9, so the empty line belongs to births from 2000 on. Run over every day, Ulune’s code finds the line empty on about 24% of dates from 2000 to 2099, and on none before. It finds it full on about 10% of dates in the 1900s, and on about 2% in the 2000s.

## In practice

Take a made-up birth date, 30 June 1981. Without the zeros its digits are 1 1 3 6 8 9. The top row holds a 3, a 6 and a 9, so the line 3-6-9 is full. Ulune lists it as “full: an arrow”. No other line of the grid is full or empty.

Now take 15 July 2001. Its digits are 1 1 2 5 7: no 3, no 6, no 9. The top row is empty, and Ulune lists the line as “empty: what is missing”. The date’s weight lies in the lower rows instead, with the two 1s and the 7 on the bottom row.

## Across traditions

The Lo Shu square is laid out 4 9 2 / 3 5 7 / 8 1 6. There, the digits 3, 6 and 9 do not stand on one line. The 9 is at the top centre, the 3 on the middle left and the 6 at the bottom right. So the Lo Shu view of Ulune has no line 3-6-9. For the date 15 July 2001, it shows another empty line instead: the left column, 4-3-8.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune counts the digits of the local date of birth, day, month and year, with the zeros set aside. The line 3-6-9 is full when the date holds at least one 3, one 6 and one 9. It is empty when the date holds none of them, and partial otherwise, shown with a dash. Repeats do not change the state: 3 3 6 9 is as full as 3 6 9. It is the first of the eight lines of Phillips’s layout, the top row, and it appears only in that layout.
