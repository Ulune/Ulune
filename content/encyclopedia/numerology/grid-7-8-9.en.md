## Meaning

The line 7-8-9 is the right column of the [[birth-grid|birth grid]] in David A. Phillips’s layout. It climbs through the three planes: [[number-7|7]] for the body, [[number-8|8]] for the soul, [[number-9|9]] for the mind. It joins learning through experience, strength and achievement, and the wide, idealistic view. These are the three largest digits, the last steps of the count.

When all three cells hold a digit, the line is full. Phillips names it the arrow of activity. The tradition reads a person who needs to be doing: busy hands, a full diary, and ideas tested in action rather than left on paper. Rest can feel hard. The gift is at its best when activity has a direction, and when some quiet is let in between the tasks.

When all three cells are empty, authors give the line different names, and Ulune keeps to the digits. The tradition reads energy that starts slowly. Such a person may wait for the right moment, or for a push from outside, before moving. Small, concrete first steps help, and so do shared projects that carry the momentum. It is a rhythm to understand, not a fault.

A partial line, with one or two of its digits, says little alone.

Every year from 1900 to 1999 holds a 9, so the empty line belongs to births from 2000 on. Run over every day, Ulune’s code finds the line empty on about 26% of dates from 2000 to 2099, and on none before. It finds it full on about 9% of dates in the 1900s, and about 2% in the 2000s.

## In practice

Take a made-up birth date, 3 November 1978. Its digits are 1 1 1 3 7 8 9. The right column holds a 7, an 8 and a 9: the line 7-8-9 is full, and Ulune lists it as “full: an arrow”. The same date leaves the centre column, [[grid-4-5-6|4-5-6]], empty.

Now take 24 March 2005. Without the zeros its digits are 2 2 3 4 5. No 7, no 8, no 9: the right column is empty, and Ulune lists the line as “empty: what is missing”.

## Across traditions

The Lo Shu square is laid out 4 9 2 / 3 5 7 / 8 1 6. There, the digits 7, 8 and 9 do not stand on one line. The 9 is at the top centre, the 7 on the middle right and the 8 at the bottom left. So the Lo Shu view of Ulune has no line 7-8-9.

For 24 March 2005, the Lo Shu shows another empty line: the bottom row, 8-1-6.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune counts the digits of the local date of birth, day, month and year, with the zeros set aside. The line 7-8-9 is full when the date holds at least one 7, one 8 and one 9. It is empty when the date holds none of them, and partial otherwise, shown with a dash. A repeated digit counts once. It is the right column of Phillips’s layout, and it appears only in that layout.
