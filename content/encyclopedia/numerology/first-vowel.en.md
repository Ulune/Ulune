## Meaning

The first vowel is the first vowel of the first name. In numerology, vowels stand for the inner life. Their sum over the whole name is the [[soul-urge|Soul Urge]], what a person wants deep down. The first vowel is the first of those letters, a small window onto the same inner side.

Ulune reads it as a glimpse of the inner self: how a person reacts when no one is looking.

Only six letters can take this place: A, E, I, O, U, and Y when it sounds as a vowel. Each has its own character, given in the table below.

The first vowel is one of the three [[stones|stones]] of the first name, with the [[cornerstone|cornerstone]] and the [[capstone|capstone]]. Sometimes the first vowel is the cornerstone itself, as in Anna or Élodie. Then the first step and the inner reaction share one letter and one character.

## In practice

Take a made-up person, Yolanda Mary Kyle, born 29 November 1984. Her first name starts with Y. Here Y comes first and stands before a vowel, so by Hans Decoz’s rule it sounds as a consonant. The first vowel is the next one, O, worth 6.

Ulune’s reading for O: principles and devotion, responsible, patient and protective, sometimes rigid about what is right. As a first vowel, numerology reads this as her private reaction. Before anything else, she would weigh what is fair and who needs looking after.

Her value 6 links the first vowel to the [[number-6|6]], the number of responsibility and care. Her cornerstone is Y and her capstone A.

In a name like Bryan, things go the other way. The Y stands between a consonant and a vowel, with no vowel before it, so it is the vowel of its syllable. Bryan’s first vowel is Y.

## Origin and history

The first vowel belongs to modern name numerology. Matthew Oliver Goodwin’s guide (1981) gives a chapter to the first letter and the first vowel, read as a person’s approach to experience. Hans Decoz, whose rules Ulune follows, lists the first vowel with the cornerstone and capstone in the book he wrote with Tom Monte (1994).

## How Ulune calculates it

- Ulune takes the first word of the full name at birth, or else of the chart’s own typed name. Accents are dropped first.
- A, E, I, O and U are always vowels. W never is.
- Each Y is set by Hans Decoz’s rule, from its neighbours in the same name. Alone, it is a vowel.
- First in the name, a Y is a vowel before a consonant (Yvonne), a consonant before a vowel (Yolanda).
- Last in the name, it is a vowel after a consonant (Barry), a consonant after a vowel (Mickey).
- Between two consonants it is a vowel (Kyle). After a vowel it goes with it, as a consonant (Taylor).
- Between a consonant and a vowel, it is a vowel when no vowel comes before it in the name (Ryan, Bryan), a consonant otherwise (Tanya).
- Each Y can be switched by hand under the wheel, and the first vowel follows.
- A first name with no vowel, such as a single initial, has no first vowel. Ulune shows “none”.
