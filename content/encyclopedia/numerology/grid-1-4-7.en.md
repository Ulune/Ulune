## Meaning

The line 1-4-7 is the bottom row of the [[birth-grid|birth grid]] in David A. Phillips’s layout. He calls it the physical plane: the base of the grid, where numerology reads action, the hands, organisation and patience. It joins [[number-1|1]], initiative, [[number-4|4]], method and steady work, and [[number-7|7]], learning through experience.

When all three cells hold a digit, the line is full. Phillips names it the arrow of practicality. The tradition reads someone at home in the material world: good with tools, plans and budgets, able to turn an idea into something that stands. The gift asks for room for feeling and ideas too, so that the practical does not crowd them out.

When all three cells are empty, the bottom row is silent. Authors give this empty line different names, and Ulune keeps to the digits. The tradition reads a person whose first home is thought or feeling rather than the body. Practical skills are then learned on purpose: by doing, step by step, with patience. Sport, crafts and simple routines help bring the attention down to earth.

A partial line, with one or two of its digits, says little alone.

Every year from 1900 to 1999 holds a 1, so the empty line belongs to births from 2000 on. Run over every day, Ulune’s code finds the line empty on about 11% of dates from 2000 to 2099, and on none before. It finds it full on about 9% of dates in the 1900s and about 5% in the 2000s.

## In practice

Take a made-up birth date, 17 April 1962. Its digits are 1 1 2 4 6 7 9. The bottom row holds two 1s, a 4 and a 7: the line 1-4-7 is full, and Ulune lists it as “full: an arrow”.

Now take 30 May 2009. Without the zeros its digits are 2 3 5 9. No 1, no 4, no 7: the bottom row is empty, and Ulune lists the line as “empty: what is missing”. The digits sit higher, in the rows of the mind and the soul.

## Across traditions

The Lo Shu square is laid out 4 9 2 / 3 5 7 / 8 1 6. There, the digits 1, 4 and 7 do not stand on one line. The 4 is at the top left, the 7 on the middle right and the 1 at the bottom centre. So the Lo Shu view of Ulune has no line 1-4-7.

The same dates read differently there. For 17 April 1962, the Lo Shu shows two other full lines, the top row 4-9-2 and the right column 2-7-6. For 30 May 2009, it shows the bottom row, 8-1-6, empty.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune counts the digits of the local date of birth, day, month and year, with the zeros set aside. The line 1-4-7 is full when the date holds at least one 1, one 4 and one 7. It is empty when the date holds none of them, and partial otherwise, shown with a dash. A repeated digit counts once. It is the bottom row of Phillips’s layout, and it appears only in that layout.
