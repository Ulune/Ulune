## Meaning

The Maturity number joins the two main numbers of a chart. One is the [[life-path|Life Path]], from the birth date; the other is the [[expression|Expression]], from the full birth name. Numerology reads it as a goal that grows clearer with age. As a person learns to put their abilities to work in the direction of their life, this number comes forward.

The tradition places that moment in the second half of life, usually from the late thirties or forties. Books differ on the exact age, and the change is read as gradual, never as a date. Before then, the Maturity number is read as a quiet undercurrent. Afterwards it is a clearer aim.

A Maturity 1 points to a second half of life that asks for more independence, perhaps starting something of one’s own. A Maturity 2 points to growth through sharing and closer relationships. A Maturity 3 leaves more room for expression; many people find their voice later rather than sooner. A Maturity 4 builds: order, security, work that lasts.

Because it adds the road and the talents, the tradition reads it as what the two make together. It is neither a new direction nor a reward. It is the shape the life tends to take once road and talents have met.

## In practice

Take a made-up person, Camille Marie Laurent, born on 15 June 1990. Her Life Path is 4 and her Expression is 3. Ulune adds them: 4 + 3 = 7. Camille has a Maturity 7. Numerology reads a second half of life turned towards understanding: study, reflection, and a deeper sense of what is true for her.

A second made-up person, Lucie Anne Moreau, born on 4 July 1993, shows how master numbers are added. Her Life Path is 33 (7 + 4 + 22) and her Expression is 13/4. Ulune adds the master number whole: 33 + 4 = 37, then 10, then 1. Lucie has a Maturity 1, a later life that asks for more independence.

In a reading, Ulune shows the Maturity number with the core numbers, with its steps: “Life Path + Expression”.

## Across traditions

- **Astrology** also marks stages of adult life, through the slow planets returning to or facing their birth places. [[saturn|Saturn]] comes back to its birth place around the age of 29. [[uranus|Uranus]] reaches the opposite point of its birth place around 40 to 42, a [[transit|transit]] often linked to mid-life. Setting these beside the Maturity number is a comparison between two languages, not a rule of either.
- **Numerology’s own long cycles** divide life too. The [[pinnacle|pinnacles]] and the [[period-cycle|period cycles]] change on given birthdays. The Maturity number has no start date: it grows.

## Origin and history

The Maturity number belongs to modern numerology. It rests on two choices made by 20th-century manuals. A name and a date each give a core number, and adding them shows what they produce together. Nothing like it appears in ancient number lore, and the Pythagorean name it borrows is a later attribution.

Ulune follows Hans Decoz, whose school calls it the Maturity number. His World Numerology site adds the Life Path and the Expression, and says the number has little influence until about the age of 35. Ulune’s own texts place it a little later, in the late thirties or forties. Both describe a slow emergence rather than a fixed date.

## How Ulune calculates it

- Ulune adds the Life Path and the Expression as they stand, master numbers included: a Life Path 11 adds 11, not 2.
- The total is reduced by adding its digits, keeping 11, 22 and 33.
- Ulune does not mark a karmic debt on the Maturity number, even when its total passes through 13, 14, 16 or 19. Karmic debts are read on the Life Path, the name numbers and the Birthday.
- The Expression comes from the full name at birth, so the Maturity number needs one. Without a name, it waits and shows a dash. A name used now, such as a married name, does not change it.
