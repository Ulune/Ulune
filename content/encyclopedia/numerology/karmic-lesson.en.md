## Meaning

In numerology each letter of the name has a value from 1 to 9: A is 1, B is 2, and so on. Counting the letters of the birth name by value shows which numbers the name holds, and how often. A karmic lesson is a number from 1 to 9 that no letter falls on.

Numerology reads a missing number as a quality that may have to be learned on purpose rather than by nature. It is not a flaw, and not a sentence. The quality is simply less familiar, so it asks for practice. Each lesson comes with a gentle way in:

- **1**: standing up for oneself and owning a decision. It grows one decision at a time.
- **2**: patience, tact and cooperation. Listening before answering is a good start.
- **3**: expressing oneself, in words, art or play. Small, regular sharing builds confidence.
- **4**: method and steady work. A simple routine helps more than good intentions.
- **5**: openness to change. Small changes make the big ones less daunting.
- **6**: responsibility towards home and community. It begins with showing up reliably for one or two people.
- **7**: depth, reflection and trust in the inner voice. Regular quiet time helps it grow.
- **8**: confidence with money, authority and practical power. Taking charge of small things builds it.
- **9**: compassion, a wider view, letting go of what has ended. Giving a little time to others is a way in.

A name can miss several numbers, or none. The lessons are read together with the rest of the chart, never alone.

## In practice

Take a made-up person, Bernard Olivier Martin. His name has 20 letters. Ulune counts them by value:

- 1: A, A. Two letters.
- 2: B, T. Two.
- 3: L. One.
- 4: D, V, M. Three.
- 5: E, N, E, N. Four.
- 6: O. One.
- 7: none.
- 8: none.
- 9: R, R, I, I, R, R, I. Seven.

No letter falls on 7 or on 8, so his karmic lessons are 7 and 8. Numerology would read two invitations here. One is to make room for quiet reflection; the other, to grow at ease with practical authority.

The same count gives two other numbers. Nine is the number with the most letters, seven of them: his [[hidden-passion|hidden passion]] is 9. And the name holds seven of the nine numbers, so his [[subconscious-self|subconscious self]] is 9 − 2 = 7.

## Across traditions

- **The birth grid.** The [[birth-grid|birth grid]] places the digits of the birth date on a square of nine. A number missing there is a gap in the date rather than in the name. Ulune shows it on the grid but does not call it a karmic lesson.
- **Karmic debts.** The [[karmic-debt|karmic debts]], 13, 14, 16 and 19, are the other karmic numbers. They come from totals, not from missing letters.

## Origin and history

Counting the letters of a name by value belongs to twentieth-century Western numerology. Florence Campbell’s *Your Days Are Numbered* (1931) already sets out such a count, which it calls an inclusion table, and speaks of karmic lessons.

The values are the [[pythagorean|Pythagorean]] ones, A = 1 to I = 9, then again from J. They honour Pythagoras, but the link between him and these values is a later attribution. Ulune follows the rules of Hans Decoz.

## How Ulune calculates it

- Ulune counts every letter of the full name at birth, all names together, by its Pythagorean value.
- Accents are dropped and ligatures spread out first: é counts as E, œ as O and E.
- Y always counts as 7, whether the name reads it as a vowel or a consonant. Switching a Y by hand changes nothing here. W always counts as 5.
- Every number from 1 to 9 with no letter is a karmic lesson. There can be several, or none.
- Only the birth name gives lessons. A name used now, if different, does not.
- The [[subconscious-self|subconscious self]] is 9 minus the number of lessons. The hidden passion is the number with the most letters; ties of up to three are all read, and four or more name none.
- On the numerology wheel, a lesson’s number shows no letter, and its reading gives the lesson’s text.
