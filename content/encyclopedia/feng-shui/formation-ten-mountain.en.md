## Meaning

The Combination of Ten for the mountain stars is one of the [[flying-star|Flying Stars]]’ formations that Ulune names, a pattern that runs through the whole of a home’s [[flying-star-chart|chart]]: in all nine palaces the mountain star and the period star add up to ten, a 1 with a 9, a 2 with an 8, a 5 with a 5.

山星合十, shān xīng hé shí, the mountain stars combine to ten. The classics read it as a chart whose stars work together, for people and the bonds between them, a harmony that runs through the whole home rather than one palace, and they let it soften what else the chart carries.

Of the 216 main charts, the twenty-four facings of each of the nine [[period|periods]], 12 carry this formation, in Periods 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9.

## In practice

- **What practitioners suggest**: nothing to change; they read the rooms of the home as carried by the pattern, and give its palaces’ own pairs their usual care.
- **In the Home lens**: the chart’s reading names its formations. Built in Period 9, a home facing [[mountain-xun|Xun]] or [[mountain-si|Si]] carries this one; built in Period 8, a home facing [[mountain-wei|Wei]].
- **Near an edge**: the replacement chart, which Ulune shows beside the main one for a facing close to the edge of its mountain, has formations of its own, named the same way; it may keep this one or not.

## Across traditions

- **The Lo Shu’s own pairs**: across the centre of the Lo Shu every two numbers make ten, 1 and 9, 2 and 8, 3 and 7, 4 and 6; the Combination of Ten gives each palace that balance within itself.
- **The He Tu**, the River Chart, pairs its numbers by five, 1 with 6, 2 with 7; the Lo Shu pairs them by ten across its centre, and the Combination of Ten follows the Lo Shu.

## Origin and history

The Combination of Ten, 合十, is one of the Xuan Kong school’s classic patterns, and a rare one: twelve of the 216 main charts. It comes with its mirror, the Combination of Ten for the water stars, across the compass, in Period 9 for a home facing Xun or Si on one side and Qian or Hai on the other.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune casts each home’s chart, then checks it against every formation it knows. For this one the rule is: in all nine palaces the mountain star and the period star add up to ten, a 1 with a 9, a 2 with an 8, a 5 with a 5. It is read on the main chart and on the replacement chart alike, each on its own. Ulune’s tests check its formations against charts worked by hand.

An example: a home facing [[mountain-xun|Xun]], built in Period 9. The period chart sets 9 in the centre; the mountain star goes to the centre as a 1 and flies backward, the water star as a 8 and flies forward. At the back, in the palace of the north-west, stand mountain star 9 and water star 9; at the front, in the palace of the south-east, mountain star 2 and water star 7. Palace by palace, mountain star and period star: centre 1 + 9, north-west 9 + 1, west 8 + 2, north-east 7 + 3, south 6 + 4, north 5 + 5, south-west 4 + 6, east 3 + 7, south-east 2 + 8.
