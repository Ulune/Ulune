## Meaning

The opposed chart is one of the [[flying-star|Flying Stars]]’ formations that Ulune names, a pattern that runs through the whole of a home’s [[flying-star-chart|chart]]: the mountain star or the water star that goes to the centre is a 5 and flies backward, so that every palace holds the number opposite its own, the 9 in the north and the 1 in the south.

反吟, fǎn yín, the opposed chart. The classics read it as energy running against the square’s own order: the stars of one kind stand across from where the square would set them, each palace holding its opposite. It is not read as a fault of the home, and practitioners give it attention mainly when the years’ stars are read on top.

Of the 216 main charts, the twenty-four facings of each of the nine [[period|periods]], 24 carry this formation, in Periods 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9.

## In practice

- **What practitioners suggest**: a little more care with the years, since the year’s own stars pass over a chart that turns the square round; the palaces are otherwise read as usual.
- **In the Home lens**: the chart’s reading names its formations. Built in Period 9, a home facing [[mountain-zi|Zi]], [[mountain-gui|Gui]], [[mountain-wu|Wu]] or [[mountain-ding|Ding]] carries this one; built in Period 8, a home facing [[mountain-chou|Chou]] or [[mountain-wei|Wei]].
- **Near an edge**: the replacement chart, which Ulune shows beside the main one for a facing close to the edge of its mountain, has formations of its own, named the same way; it may keep this one or not.

## Across traditions

- **Words of divination**: in Qimen Dunjia and Da Liu Ren, 反吟 names a plate set opposite its own places, as 伏吟 names one set on them; the Flying Stars keep both words for the 5’s two flights.
- **Never by the year**: the year’s and the month’s charts always fly forward, so they can repeat the Lo Shu but never turn it round; the opposed chart belongs to a home’s own stars alone.

## Origin and history

The opposed chart is the repeated chart’s mirror: the same 5 in the centre, the other way round. Twenty-four of the 216 main charts carry it, in mirrored pairs of periods; in the present Period 9 it falls to a home facing Zi, Gui, Wu or Ding, the middle and last mountains of the north and the south.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune casts each home’s chart, then checks it against every formation it knows. For this one the rule is: the mountain star or the water star that goes to the centre is a 5 and flies backward, so that every palace holds the number opposite its own, the 9 in the north and the 1 in the south. It counts only when a 5 flies in the centre as the mountain or the water star, the 5 flying by the home’s own mountain since it has no palace. Ulune’s tests check its formations against charts worked by hand.

An example: a home facing [[mountain-zi|Zi]], built in Period 9. The period chart sets 9 in the centre; the mountain star goes to the centre as a 4 and flies forward, the water star as a 5 and flies backward. At the back, in the palace of the south, stand mountain star 8 and water star 1; at the front, in the palace of the north, mountain star 9 and water star 9. The 5 here is the water star; palace by palace, those stars: centre 5, north-west 4, west 3, north-east 2, south 1, north 9, south-west 8, east 7, south-east 6.
