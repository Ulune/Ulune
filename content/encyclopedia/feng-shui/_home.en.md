Feng Shui, 風水 fēngshuǐ, means “wind and water”. The name comes from the Book of Burial attributed to Guo Pu (276–324): qi, the breath of things, rides the wind and scatters, and stops where it meets water, so the ancients sought places where it gathers and does not scatter, and called the art of finding them wind and water. It is the Chinese tradition of how places and directions suit the people who live in them.

Ulune reads Feng Shui in two halves. The first belongs to a person: from the year of birth and the tradition’s yin or yang count comes a Kua number, one of the eight trigrams, and from the Kua the Eight Mansions, four directions that support and four that ask for more care, laid out on a compass rose. The second belongs to a home: its facing direction, measured with a compass, falls in one of the 24 mountains; with the period in which the home was built or moved into, it gives the home’s Flying Star chart, nine numbers moving through nine palaces, read palace by palace with the stars of the year and the month.

Two schools meet here. The Form School, which tradition traces to Yang Yunsong in the 9th century, reads the shape of the land, the mountains behind and the waters before; the Compass School reads directions and time with the luopan, the Chinese compass. The Eight Mansions follow the Ba Zhai Ming Jing, a Qing-dynasty manual, and the Flying Stars the method set out by Shen Zhureng (1849–1906), whose work was published after his death. Ulune’s method notes say at each step which convention it chose.

What Ulune suggests is always a way of arranging and using a place, where to sit, which room to give to rest or to work, never anything to buy. A home you give is kept only in your private space, encrypted on your device, and nothing about it is ever sent.

The foundations Feng Shui reads are in [[chinese|Chinese cosmology]]: the [[phase|five phases]], which give every direction and every star its colour, and [[yin-yang|yin and yang]], which set the stars’ flight; the trigrams and the [[lo-shu|Lo Shu]] square join them there. Feng Shui’s own pages, the Kua numbers and the Eight Mansions first, are written next.
