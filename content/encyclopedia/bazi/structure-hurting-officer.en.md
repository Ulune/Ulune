## Meaning

The Hurting Officer structure is the frame of a chart whose month brings the [[ten-god-hurting-officer|Hurting Officer]], the phase the Day Master produces, of the other polarity. The name sounds harsh and reads otherwise: shāngguān, “hurting the officer”, says only that this output controls the Day Master’s Direct Officer, the god of rules. Ulune’s reading puts the structure in a sentence: it frames a life around brilliance and freedom, originality, a sharp tongue and mind, and the urge to question how things are done.

The tradition reads a bright, independent mind: someone who sees what does not work and says so, invents, performs, reforms, and is seldom content with “it has always been done this way”. Such a chart often has a gift for words, for the arts and for every craft where originality counts more than obedience.

Its difficulty is the edge itself: friction with rules and with those who keep them, words that cut before they persuade. The classics never asked it to be quiet, only to be given a discipline and a use, and Ulune reads the structure as a voice to channel, never as a conflict foretold.

## In practice

- **When Ulune names it**: the month branch holds the Day Master’s Hurting Officer and it comes first, shown among the stems before the month’s other hidden stems, or as the month’s main qi, peers aside, when nothing else shows.
- **Its months, by main qi**: Jia in Wu, Yi in Yin or Si, Bing in Chou or Wei, Ding in Chen, Si or Xu, Wu in You or Xu, Ji in Shen, Geng in Zi, Xin in Shen or Hai, Ren in Mao, Gui in Yin or Hai.
- **What the classics look for**: the [[ten-god-direct-resource|Direct Resource]], which controls it, the books’ “Hurting Officer wearing the seal”, brilliance given a discipline; or Wealth, which it produces, “the Hurting Officer producing wealth”, talent put to work; or Seven Killings, which it can master. They watch its meeting with the [[ten-god-direct-officer|Direct Officer]], the critic face to face with the rule.
- **With strength**: output draws on the Day Master; for a strong one the Hurting Officer is an outlet, for a weak one the classics prefer the Resource that both feeds the self and tempers the edge. Ulune shows the strength beside the structure.
- **In time**, a decade of the Hurting Officer is one of originality and change, speaking up, questioning, creating, as the luck pillars’ readings say: a time to choose your battles and to channel the spark.

## Across traditions

- **In Japan and Korea** the structure is the shōkan-kaku of Shichū Suimei and the sanggwan-gyeok of saju.
- **In Western astrology** the closest theme is a sharp Mercury with Uranus, the planet of the unconventional: the same gift for seeing what others miss, read from planets rather than from the month.

## Origin and history

No structure divides the classics more. The Yuanhai Ziping of the Song names it beside the Eating God, and the Ming books, the San Ming Tong Hui first, give it whole sections, with its famous warning, the Hurting Officer meeting the Officer, and its famous praise, the Hurting Officer wearing the seal. Shen Xiaozhan’s Ziping Zhenquan, in the 18th century, counts it among the four forceful gods to be tempered rather than suppressed, and writes that it is, for all its edge, the brilliant part of a chart, which is why scholars and writers so often have it. The Di Tian Sui, with Ren Tieqiao’s commentary, weighs it by the balance of the whole chart, and Xu Lewu’s commentary of 1936 kept both readings.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune names the Hurting Officer structure when, after the Prosperity and Sword Edge tests, the first of the month branch’s non-peer hidden stems to show among the stems of the year, month and hour is the Day Master’s Hurting Officer, or, if none shows, when that stem is the first of them. The Hurting Officer is the stem of the phase the Day Master produces, of the other polarity: Ding for Jia, Bing for Yi, Ji for Bing, Wu for Ding, Xin for Wu, Geng for Ji, Gui for Geng, Ren for Xin, Yi for Ren and Jia for Gui.
