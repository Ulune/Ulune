## Meaning

The Golden Carriage is a star of comfort and grace: ease, good taste, and help from those close, from a partner above all. Its name, 金輿 jīnyú, is the gilded carriage of a high official, the vehicle that went with rank; in the tables it rides two branches ahead of the Day Master’s stipend, the [[shen-sha-prosperity-star|Prosperity star]], as a carriage goes before the one it carries.

The tradition reads a life made easier by the people around it: a partner who brings support and refinement, a household that looks after its own, a natural feeling for beauty and for doing things well. A Day Master with its Golden Carriage in a pillar is read as finding that comfort in the part of life the pillar stands for, and, in the day branch, the [[partner-palace|partner palace]], the classics read it most of all as a partner’s gifts.

It is a gentle star and a secondary one. Ulune reads it as grace and as help received from those close, never as wealth or a marriage promised, and as a reminder that comfort shared with others is the kind the star describes.

## In practice

- **Found from the Day Master**: Jia in Chen, Yi in Si, Bing and Wu in Wei, Ding and Ji in Shen, Geng in Xu, Xin in Hai, Ren in Chou and Gui in Yin, sought in every pillar, the day’s own included.
- **Two branches after the stipend**: each is the Prosperity star’s branch moved on by two, Yin to Chen for Jia, Mao to Si for Yi.
- **With the life stages**: for a yang Day Master the carriage stands in its [[life-stage-decline|Decline]] branch, seasoned strength; for a yin one in its [[life-stage-bath|Bath]] branch, the stage of charm.
- **On the board**, the star is listed with the pillars whose branches hold it, and its reading says which branch the Day Master looks for.
- **In time**, a luck pillar or a year whose branch is the carriage brings the star for its while; the full table shows it in that row.

## Across traditions

- **In imperial China** the carriage was a mark of rank, its colours and gilding fixed by the court for each degree of office; the star borrows that image of a life carried in some comfort.
- **In Western astrology** comfort, taste and the help of a partner belong to Venus and to the seventh house, the house of partners: the same qualities, read through a planet and a place rather than through a branch.

## Origin and history

The Golden Carriage comes from the tables of the Song and Ming books of the Zi Ping school, the Yuanhai Ziping and the San Ming Tong Hui, which teach it by a verse: “Jia the Dragon, Yi the Snake, Bing and Wu the Goat; Ding and Ji sing to the Monkey, Geng goes to the Dog; Xin the Pig, Ren the Ox, Gui meets the Tiger.” The rule behind the verse is the stipend two branches back, which ties the carriage to the Prosperity star and so to the Day Master’s own ground. Popular almanacs read it chiefly for marriage; Ulune keeps the wider reading the classics also give, comfort, grace and help from those close.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune finds the Golden Carriage from the Day Master, two branches after its Prosperity star: Chen, Si, Wei, Shen, Wei, Shen, Xu, Hai, Chou and Yin, from Jia to Gui. It looks for that branch in every pillar, the day’s own included, and in the branch of a luck pillar or a year. The star is shown with the pillars it falls in, with its reading and the branch the Day Master looks for.
