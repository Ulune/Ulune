## Meaning

The Canopy is the star of solitude and the spirit: art, philosophy, a taste for the deep and the unusual, time spent alone with ideas. Its name, 華蓋 huágài, is the ornate canopy held over an emperor or carried before a deity, and also a group of stars near the pole that spreads over the Purple Palace, the emperor’s quarter of the sky. It shelters, and it sets apart.

The tradition reads an inward, contemplative gift: a mind drawn to what lies beneath the surface, a talent for art or study that grows in quiet, an independence that is happy with its own company. The classics associated it with monks, Daoists and artists, the people who live a little apart in order to see further. A pillar holding the Canopy is read as the part of life where that reflective nature shows; in a luck pillar or a year, as a time for study, retreat or creative work.

Its other face is a solitude that becomes distance, a depth that forgets to come back to the surface and share what it found. The tradition values a Canopy that gives its fruits to others, and Ulune reads it as a gift for depth, never as loneliness foretold.

## In practice

- **Found from the year and day branches**, by the trine each belongs to, and looked for in the other pillars: Chen for the Water trine, Shen, Zi and Chen; Wei for the Wood trine, Hai, Mao and Wei; Xu for the Fire trine, Yin, Wu and Xu; Chou for the Metal trine, Si, You and Chou.
- **The storehouse of the trine**: each Canopy is the last branch of its trine, the [[life-stage-tomb|Storehouse]] of its phase, where what has grown is gathered in: a fitting branch for a star of reflection.
- **On the board**, the star is listed with the pillars whose branches hold it; its reading says from which pillar it was found and of which trine.
- **In time**, a luck pillar or a year whose branch is the Canopy brings the star for its while; the full table shows it in that row.
- **Its companions**: the [[shen-sha-peach-blossom|Peach Blossom]], the [[shen-sha-travelling-horse|Travelling Horse]] and the [[shen-sha-general|General]] are found the same way, from the same [[relation-trine|trines]].

## Across traditions

- **In Japan, Korea and Vietnam** the star is kagai, hwagae and Hoa Cái; in Korea hwagaesal names an artistic, solitary temperament, and in Vietnam Hoa Cái is a star of Tử Vi as well.
- **In Chinese astronomy** the Canopy is an asterism above the pole, which the old star lists describe as the umbrella over the throne of the sky.
- **In Western astrology** retreat, contemplation and the inner life belong to the twelfth house and, for modern astrologers, to Neptune: the same quiet depth, read from a place and a planet rather than from a branch.

## Origin and history

The canopy over the throne gave its name to stars before it gave it to a sign: Sima Qian’s treatise on the heavens, around 94 BC, already places stars over the Purple Palace, and the later star catalogues name the Huagai asterism among them. The symbolic star belongs to the “three lives” method and to the Zi Ping school that kept it; the Yuanhai Ziping and the San Ming Tong Hui give its rule, the storehouse branch of each trine, and its traditional reading, a lofty and solitary spirit, gifted for the arts and for the religious life.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune reads the Canopy from two bases, the year branch and the day branch. For each it takes the branch’s trine and its Canopy, Chen, Wei, Xu or Chou, and looks for it in the other pillars: the year’s Canopy in the month, day and hour, the day’s in the year, month and hour. For a luck pillar or a year, it looks for either in its branch. The star is shown with the pillars it falls in, and its reading says which base found it.
