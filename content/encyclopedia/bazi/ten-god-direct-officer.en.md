## Meaning

The Direct Officer is a stem of the phase that controls the [[day-master|Day Master]], of the other polarity: Xin for a Jia Day Master, Geng for a Yi, Yi for a Wu. It belongs to power, the family of what shapes and tests the self, beside [[ten-god-seven-killings|Seven Killings]]; but where Seven Killings presses as one polarity on the same, the Direct Officer governs as a yin over a yang or a yang over a yin, a control with a measure in it.

Its name, zhèngguān, is “the proper officer”, and it carries the ideal of the old official: the post held by merit, the rule applied fairly, the good name kept. The tradition reads in it order, duty and reputation, and the self-respect of keeping one’s word. Ulune reads it as the Zi Ping school’s consensus does: order, duty and reputation, fairness and the respect of rules. Its caution is the weight of the frame: where the Officer is heavy and the self light, the classics look for room to breathe within the rules.

Like all power, it draws on the Day Master: a strong Day Master is steadied by it, given a structure in which its strength serves; a weak one finds it a demand, and the classics look to the resource, which the Officer produces and which supports the self.

## In practice

- **On the board**: for a Jia Day Master, a Xin stem among the pillars, or the Xin hidden in You, Xu and Chou.
- **Its reading**: shown, a sense of order, duty and fairness, and a good name earned by keeping your word; only in the branches, a private conscience, rules kept for yourself rather than shown; absent, you may live more by your own rules, and the tradition suggests building structures you choose.
- **Strength and balance**: power draws on the Day Master; when the Day Master is strong, power is among the phases that bring balance.
- **The structure**: when the month’s structure comes from its Direct Officer, revealed among the stems or as the month’s main qi, Ulune names the Direct Officer structure (正官格).
- **Luck pillars and years**: a decade of the Direct Officer is one of structure, of roles, duties and recognition, a time to build a good name within a framework; a year, one of order and recognition, with responsibilities and commitments to keep.
- **Two people**: when the other’s Day Master is your Direct Officer, theirs controls yours: they bring structure and challenge, you rise to it, a bond that shapes, at its best when the pressure is kind.

## Across traditions

- **In Japan, Korea and Vietnam** it keeps its name: seikan in Shichū Suimei, jeonggwan in Korean saju, chính quan in Vietnamese.
- **In Western astrology** its themes belong to Saturn and to the tenth house, of vocation, standing and the rules one keeps: the same matters, read by a planet and a house rather than by a relation of phases.

## Origin and history

The Direct Officer is the god the old books honour first. In a world where an official post was the measure of a life, the Yuanhai Ziping and the Ming classics read the proper officer as the sign of a good name and an orderly career, and the Ziping Zhenquan, under the Qing, opens its chapters on the structures with it. Its favourite pairings follow the cycles: “wealth produces the Officer”, means supporting standing, and “the Officer and the Resource produce each other”, the Officer producing the [[ten-god-direct-resource|Direct Resource]], which supports the self, so that duty turns into protection.

The same books name what troubles it: the Hurting Officer, which controls it, the critic against the rule, and a chart where the Officer stands mixed with Seven Killings, two powers pulling at once, for the classics prefer one clear authority. The Di Tian Sui reminds its readers that order serves the self only when the self can carry it.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune names a stem the Direct Officer when its phase is three steps from the Day Master’s along the generating cycle, the phase that controls the Day Master, and their polarities differ. It looks among the stems of the year, the month and the hour and among the hidden stems of the branches with their weights, counts the Direct Officer shown and hidden, and reads it as shown, hidden or absent. Power draws on the Day Master in the strength tests, and the month gives the Direct Officer structure when the Direct Officer is the first of the month branch’s hidden stems, peers aside, to show among the stems, or, if none shows, the first of them.
