## Meaning

The Rest is the eighth of the twelve [[life-stage|life stages]], after the Retreat. Its phase comes to rest here, its outward strength stilled, like a field after the harvest: nothing grows on it now, and nothing needs to. The classics call the stage 死 sǐ, “Death”, after the human life their twelve names follow, and the Ziping Zhenquan explains it as the phase’s qi spent, with nothing left over. The name tells how strong the phase is in this branch, never an event, and nothing about anyone’s life or health: Ulune calls the stage the Rest.

The tradition reads depth, focus and a mind that goes to the heart of things. A Day Master at its Rest stage in a pillar’s branch spends little energy outwardly there; what it has goes inward, into attention, concentration and a quiet persistence. It is the stage of the specialist and the scholar more than of the performer.

Its difficulty is stillness held too long: a reluctance to move, a seriousness that forgets to play. The tradition values what the stage offers without asking it to be something else, and notes that rest is a beginning in disguise: where the yang stems rest, the yin stems are born.

## In practice

- **For each Day Master**: Jia rests in Wu, Bing and Wu in You, Geng in Zi and Ren in Mao; Yi in Hai, Ding and Ji in Yin, Xin in Si and Gui in Shen.
- **Rest and birth exchanged**: each yang stem rests where its yin twin is born, Jia in Wu where Yi begins, Geng in Zi where Xin begins; and each yin stem rests where its yang twin is born, Yi in Hai, where Jia begins. The two cycles hand over to each other in the same branches.
- **Before and after**: the Rest follows the [[life-stage-sickness|Retreat]] and leads to the [[life-stage-tomb|Storehouse]]; across the wheel, in the clashing branch, stands its [[life-stage-bath|Bath]], the stage of display opposite the stage of depth.
- **On the board**, the stage shows under each pillar whose branch is the Day Master’s Rest branch; its reading gives the classical name with its characters and pinyin, and says what it means: the phase’s strength, nothing more.
- **In time**, a luck pillar or a year whose branch is the Rest branch is read in the same calm way, as a time when the Day Master’s phase works inwardly, good for depth rather than display.

## Across traditions

- **In Japan, Korea and Vietnam** the stage keeps its classical name, shi, sa and Tử, read as a phase’s strength.
- **In Europe’s farming year** the fallow, a field left unsown for a season so that it can bear again, is the same picture the tradition chose: rest as part of fertility, not its end.

## Origin and history

The Rest belongs to the twelve stages of the Zi Ping school, set out for the ten stems in the Yuanhai Ziping of the Song and the San Ming Tong Hui of the Ming. The Ziping Zhenquan, in the 18th century, calls the twelve names borrowed images and explains this one as qi exhausted; in the same chapter it shows the exchange that makes the stage a turning point, yang and yin each born where the other comes to rest, the natural movement of the two. Ulune’s readings first described the stage by the season, the field after the harvest; part 20 of its languages plan made Rest its name and kept 死 sǐ with its meaning in the stage’s reading and on this page.

## How Ulune calculates it

The Rest is stage 7 of the count, seven branches from the Birth: forward for a yang stem, so Jia, born in Hai, rests in Wu; backward for a yin stem, so Yi, born in Wu, rests in Hai. Earth follows Fire, Wu resting in You with Bing and Ji in Yin with Ding. The exchange of the twins follows from the table itself: a yang stem’s Rest and its yin twin’s Birth are the same branch for all five pairs, and so are a yin stem’s Rest and its yang twin’s Birth. The board shows the stage under each pillar whose branch it is, and the full table in the luck pillar and the year too.
