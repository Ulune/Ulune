## Meaning

The Direct Officer structure is the frame of a chart whose month brings the [[ten-god-direct-officer|Direct Officer]], the phase that controls the Day Master with the other polarity. Ulune’s reading puts it in a sentence: it frames a life around order and trust, duty, fairness, a good name, and a place within the rules that others can count on. Of all the [[structure|structures]] it is the one the classics placed first, the gentlest form of control, a structure that shapes the self as a good institution shapes those who serve it.

The tradition reads reliability and a sense of what is fair: someone who keeps their word, works well within a framework, and earns respect by consistency rather than display. Such a chart is often at ease with responsibility, recognition and the long view, and gives others the security of knowing where they stand.

Its difficulty is a respect for rules that can become caution, or a concern for reputation that weighs on spontaneity. The tradition looks for what keeps the frame alive, a cause that deserves the loyalty, and Ulune reads the structure as a theme, never as a career or a station promised.

## In practice

- **When Ulune names it**: the month branch holds the Day Master’s Direct Officer and it comes first, either because it shows among the stems before the month’s other hidden stems do, or because it is the month’s main qi, peers aside, and nothing else shows.
- **Its months, by main qi**: Jia in You, Yi in Shen, Bing in Zi, Ding in Hai, Wu in Mao or Chen, Ji in Yin, Geng in Wu, Xin in Si, Ren in Chou or Wei, Gui in Chen or Xu. In other months it can still name the structure when its stem shows first among the stems.
- **What the classics look for**: Wealth, which feeds the Officer, and the [[ten-god-direct-resource|Direct Resource]], which the Officer produces and which protects the self; they keep the Officer apart from the [[ten-god-hurting-officer|Hurting Officer]], which questions it, and from Seven Killings, which crowds it.
- **With strength**: a strong or balanced Day Master carries the Officer well; a weak one leans on the Resource to turn the pressure into support. Ulune shows the strength beside the structure.
- **In time**, a decade or a year of the Direct Officer brings structure, roles and recognition, as the readings of the luck pillars and the years say.

## Across traditions

- **In Japan and Korea** the structure is the seikan-kaku of Shichū Suimei and the jeonggwan-gyeok of saju, read in the same spirit.
- **In Western astrology** the closest theme is Saturn well placed and the tenth house, of duty, standing and the structures one serves: the same order and trust, drawn from a planet and a place rather than from the month.

## Origin and history

The Direct Officer is the oldest of the honoured structures. The Yuanhai Ziping of the Song already treats the Officer as the most precious of the gods for a chart of the official class, and the San Ming Tong Hui gathers the sayings around it. Shen Xiaozhan’s Ziping Zhenquan, in the 18th century, opens its chapters on the structures with the Officer: a good god, to be supported, which wants Wealth and the Resource beside it and fears the Hurting Officer that “sees” it. Xu Lewu’s 20th-century commentary, and Ulune with it, read those old preferences as a picture of a well-ordered life rather than as a rank to attain.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune names the Direct Officer structure when, after the Prosperity and Sword Edge tests, the first of the month branch’s non-peer hidden stems to show among the stems of the year, month and hour is the Day Master’s Direct Officer, or, if none shows, when that stem is the first of them. The Direct Officer is the stem of the phase that controls the Day Master’s, of the other polarity: Xin for Jia, Geng for Yi, Gui for Bing, Ren for Ding, Yi for Wu, Jia for Ji, Ding for Geng, Bing for Xin, Ji for Ren and Wu for Gui.
