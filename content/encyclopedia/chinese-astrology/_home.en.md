Chinese astrology, 生肖 shēngxiào, reads a birth through the twelve [[animal|animals]]: the Rat, the Ox, the Tiger, the Rabbit, the Dragon, the Snake, the Horse, the Goat, the Monkey, the Rooster, the Dog and the Pig. Each is one of the twelve [[branch|Earthly Branches]] of [[chinese|Chinese cosmology]], seen as an animal, and each comes back every twelve years, every twelve months, every twelve days and every twelve double hours.

Most people know their year animal (“I’m a Horse”), and it says how one meets the world. Ulune reads four: the animal of the [[year-animal|year]], of the [[month-animal|month]], of the [[day-animal|day]] and of the [[hour-animal|hour]] of a birth, as the four pillars of [[bazi|BaZi]] give them. The month animal is the inner one, the day animal the self one shows to those closest, the hour animal the secret one, kept for oneself; together they make a portrait that two people born in the same year do not share.

Each animal carries a phase of its own, the Horse Fire, the Rat Water, and a polarity, and each year adds the phase of its stem: a person born in 1990 is a Metal Horse, one born in 2026 a Fire Horse. The animals meet one another in set ways, six [[relation-harmony|harmonies]], [[relation-trine|trines]] of three, [[relation-clash|clashes]] across the circle, which say where two people, or a person and a year, find each other easily and where they ask for more care. The pages also read the year ahead, its ruler [[tai-sui|Tai Sui]] and [[ben-ming-nian|one’s own year]], the year of one’s own animal, and the animals of two people side by side.

The twelve animals appear in the day books of the Qin dynasty found at Shuihudi (about 217 BC), some of them different from today’s; Wang Chong’s Lunheng, in the 1st century, is the first text to give all twelve as they are known now. Ulune counts the animal’s year from Lichun, about 4 February, as the solar calendar and BaZi do, and says so when the lunar New Year would give another animal. What it says of an animal is the tradition’s portrait, offered as something to recognise or not, never as a fate.

The foundations these pages read, from the [[phase|five phases]] and [[yin-yang|yin and yang]] to the [[stem|Heavenly Stems]], are in Chinese cosmology; the twelve animals and the four places they are read in have their pages here.
