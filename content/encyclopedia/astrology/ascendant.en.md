## Meaning

The Ascendant is the degree of the zodiac rising on the eastern horizon at the moment and place of birth. As the Earth turns, every degree rises once a day, roughly one every four minutes, so the Ascendant changes sign about every two hours on average: of all the chart, it most needs an exact birth time.

It opens the houses: in most systems it is the cusp of the [[house-1|1st house]], and the others are counted on from it. Astrologers read it as the way someone meets the world: manner and bearing, the body and its look, first reactions, the door through which a life is entered. The rising sign is often more visible to others than the Sun sign.

The planet that rules the rising sign is the chart ruler, and its sign, house and aspects show where that approach to life is directed. For Scorpio, Aquarius and Pisces rising, traditional astrology names Mars, Saturn and Jupiter, modern astrology Pluto, Uranus and Neptune.

Opposite lies the [[descendant]], the setting degree; together they make the horizon, the axis of self and other.

## In practice

- **The rising sign** describes a manner: Aries rising often walks in quickly and speaks first, Libra rising reads the room and puts others at ease.
- **A planet near the Ascendant** colours the whole personality and is often among the first things others notice, such as Saturn’s reserve or Jupiter’s warmth.
- **Short and long ascension.** In the northern hemisphere the signs from Capricorn to Gemini rise quickly and those from Cancer to Sagittarius slowly: at the latitude of Paris, Aries and Pisces rise in about an hour, Virgo and Libra in nearly three. The southern hemisphere reverses this, so some rising signs are far more common than others in a given place.

## Across traditions

- **Indian astrology** builds the chart from the rising sign, the lagna. Its sidereal zodiac stands about 24° back, so a tropical Ascendant in the first 24° or so of a sign gives a lagna in the sign before.
- **BaZi** has no Ascendant, but its hour pillar is likewise the part of the chart that needs the birth time.
- **Human Design** uses neither the Ascendant nor the houses.

## Origin and history

Hellenistic astrologers called the rising degree the hōroskopos, the “hour-marker”, and the word passed to the whole chart, our horoscope. They made its sign the first place, the place of life, and with the other three angles the pivots of the chart, kentra in Greek and cardines in Latin. Vettius Valens and Firmicus Maternus read the body and character from it and from its ruler, and William Lilly described a person’s appearance from the rising sign and the planets close to it.

## How Ulune calculates it

The Ascendant comes from the Swiss Ephemeris’s house calculation: from the sidereal time at the birthplace, its latitude and the tilt of the ecliptic, it is the point where the ecliptic crosses the eastern horizon. In Placidus houses, Ulune’s default, as in the other quadrant systems and in equal houses, it is the cusp of the 1st house; with whole-sign houses the 1st house is the whole rising sign, and with Morinus houses the first cusp is not the Ascendant at all. Inside the polar circles, where Placidus and Koch cannot be built, Ulune uses Porphyry houses and says so.

Aspects to the Ascendant use 8° for the conjunction, square and opposition, but 6° for the trine and 4° for the sextile. Ulune names the chart ruler both ways, traditional and modern. In the weighted balance the Ascendant counts three times; without a birth time it is only a noon stand-in, marked approximate and left out of the balance. In progressions it advances with the Midheaven at the Naibod rate in right ascension, and in the calendar a transit to the Descendant is listed once, as the matching transit to the Ascendant.
