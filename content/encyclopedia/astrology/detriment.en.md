## Meaning

A planet is in its detriment in the sign opposite one it rules. Mars, at home in Aries and Scorpio, is in detriment in Libra and Taurus. Venus, at home in Taurus and Libra, is in detriment in Scorpio and Aries. The French name, *exil*, says it with an image: the planet is far from home.

Each of these signs belongs to a planet of contrary temper. The signs of the two lights face the signs of Saturn. Venus’s signs face Mars’s, and Mercury’s face Jupiter’s. A planet in detriment must work with the tools of a host whose values run against its own.

Tradition reads this as a weakness. The planet acts awkwardly, with little support, or in ways that do not suit it. William Lilly counts detriment as the heaviest debility, as heavy as [[domicile]] is strong.

Many astrologers today add a gentler reading. A planet in detriment often learns its craft the long way. What never comes automatically can become a real, conscious skill, and some of the most thoughtful uses of a planet come from this place.

## In practice

- **Venus in Scorpio**: affection felt deeply and kept private, given whole or not at all.
- **The Moon in Capricorn**: feelings held in reserve, care shown through reliability and practical help.
- **The Sun in Aquarius**: a sense of self found in groups, ideas and causes rather than in personal display.
- **Jupiter in Virgo**: faith and breadth tested by detail and doubt, and a wisdom built from many small, careful facts.
- **Other factors weigh too.** A planet in detriment may hold its own [[term]] or [[face]], stand on an angle, or be helped by aspects or by [[reception|mutual reception]]. Tradition never judges a planet by one dignity alone.

## Across traditions

- **Indian astrology** has no exact match. It weakens a planet in its debilitation, the sign opposite its exaltation, much like Western [[fall]]. It also weakens it in the signs of planets counted as its enemies.
- **Psychological astrology** tends to read detriment as a tension to work with, a planet asked to grow beyond its first habits, rather than as a flaw.

## Origin and history

The idea follows from the domiciles that Ptolemy explains in the *Tetrabiblos*. If a sign is a planet’s home, the sign opposite is the farthest place from it. The English word comes from the Latin *detrimentum*, harm or loss, used by medieval astrologers, while French astrologers say *exil*.

Lilly, in *Christian Astrology* (1647), gives detriment minus five points in his table of debilities. It is the exact counterweight of the five points of domicile. When modern rulers were given to the outer planets, new detriments followed: Uranus in Leo, Neptune in Virgo and Pluto in Taurus.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune scores detriment for the seven traditional planets, with the traditional rulers. A planet in the sign opposite one of its domiciles is in detriment and loses five of Lilly’s points. Any dignity it holds at the same degree is added. Venus between 6° and 12° Aries is in detriment but in its own term, so it scores −5 + 2 = −3.

The Dignities table names, for each degree, the planet that would be in detriment there. The wheel’s dignity overlay marks a planet in detriment with a small d. Ulune never also calls such a planet [[peregrine]], even when it holds no dignity at all. Uranus, Neptune and Pluto are not scored.
