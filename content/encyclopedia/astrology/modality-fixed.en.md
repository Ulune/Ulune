## Meaning

Fixed is one of the three modes, or modalities, of the zodiac, with [[modality-cardinal|cardinal]] and [[modality-mutable|mutable]]. The fixed signs, [[taurus]], [[leo]], [[scorpio]] and [[aquarius]], fall in the middle of each season, when it is most fully itself, and astrologers read them as persistence: holding, deepening, consolidating, seeing things through.

Each element holds in its own way. Taurus keeps resources and comforts, Leo keeps a warm and loyal heart, Scorpio keeps feelings and commitments, Aquarius keeps faith with an idea or a group. A chart with many planets in fixed signs tends to be steady, loyal and hard to deflect; its gift is endurance, the strength to finish and to protect what has been built. Its difficulty is the same firmness when change is needed: stubbornness, resistance, holding on too long.

The four fixed signs stand at right angles to one another, two pairs of opposites crossed by squares. Planets spread across them, in a [[t-square|T-square]] or a [[grand-cross|grand cross]], are often read as great determination, which grows easier when it learns when to yield.

## In practice

- **A fixed emphasis** in the balance of a chart describes staying power and constancy, and a certain reluctance to change course.
- **A fixed Sun, Moon or Ascendant** brings that quality to identity, to needs or to the first impression.
- **The succedent houses**, the 2nd, 5th, 8th and 11th, which follow the angles, are often compared with the fixed signs in modern practice.

## Across traditions

- **Jyotish** calls these signs sthira, fixed or stable, the same grouping under another name.
- **The Chinese calendar** begins its four seasons in the middle of the fixed signs: Lìchūn, the start of spring, at 15° Aquarius; Lìxià, the start of summer, at 15° Taurus; Lìqiū, the start of autumn, at 15° Leo; Lìdōng, the start of winter, at 15° Scorpio.

## Origin and history

Ptolemy called these signs solid, because when the Sun is in them the season it brings is firmly established and felt most fully. Latin authors called them fixed, and Lilly writes of fixed signs too. The middles of the four fixed signs, at 15°, are the halfway points between the equinoxes and the solstices, the cross-quarter points of the year.
