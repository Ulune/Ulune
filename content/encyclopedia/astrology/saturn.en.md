## Meaning

Saturn is the farthest planet easily seen with the naked eye, the edge of the old seven-planet sky. It takes about 29½ years to go round the zodiac, some two and a half years in each sign, and it is retrograde for about four and a half months a year.

It stands for structure, limits, time and responsibility: what has to be built slowly, what lasts, and the patience and discipline that make competence. It is also where fear, doubt and a sense of not being enough can sit, and where they are worked on over years.

Tradition calls it the greater malefic, because it restricts, delays and weighs, yet the same limits give a life its shape and make what it builds endure. It belongs to the day sect, rules [[capricorn]] and [[aquarius]] and is exalted in [[libra]]; its detriment is Cancer and Leo, its fall Aries. Modern astrologers give Aquarius to [[uranus]] and keep Saturn for Capricorn.

Its gifts are seriousness, patience, endurance and integrity. Its difficulties are rigidity, pessimism, harshness towards oneself or others, and the feeling of carrying more than one’s share.

## In practice

- **The Saturn return**, when Saturn comes back to its birth place around ages 29 and 59, is widely read as a time of stocktaking: plans borrowed from others are reconsidered, and adult commitments take their own shape.
- **Its house** shows where effort is asked for and mastery comes late: in the [[house-10|10th]], career and authority; in the 4th, a home built with care.
- **Sect matters**: tradition finds Saturn more constructive in a day chart and harder to carry by night.
- **Its transits** last months and are often exact three times because of its retrograde loops; they are read as periods of effort, restructuring and consolidation.

## Across traditions

- **Chinese astronomy** calls it 土星 Tǔxīng, the Earth star, after the five phases.
- **Indian astrology** names it Shani, son of the Sun in myth, slow and exacting, and exalts it in Libra, as Western astrology does.
- **Human Design** makes Saturn the judge: its gate shows where discipline pays off, and where things go wrong when its theme is ignored.

## Origin and history

Babylon linked the planet to Ninurta. The Greeks called it Phainon, “the bright one”, and gave it to Kronos, the father who devoured his children yet reigned over a golden age; the Roman Saturnus kept both faces, and his day, dies Saturni, survives in Saturday. Ptolemy describes Saturn as cooling and drying, Vettius Valens ties it to hardship, restraint and old age, and Marsilio Ficino linked it to melancholy but also to contemplation and deep study. Galileo saw odd “ears” beside it through his telescope in 1610, which Christiaan Huygens explained as a ring in 1659. Liz Greene’s book of 1976 helped psychological astrology reread Saturn as a teacher rather than a punishment.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune takes Saturn from the Swiss Ephemeris (DE441): its apparent position seen from the centre of the Earth, in the tropical zodiac. It is marked retrograde when its daily motion runs backwards, stationary within about five days of a station, and swift or slow against its mean motion of about 2′ a day. It is in sect in a day chart, its dignities are scored in Lilly’s points, and the Mars–Saturn midpoint is one of the five midpoints Ulune lists.

The calendar dates each pass of a Saturn transit, the Saturn return included, and shows the whole span during which Saturn stays within a degree of the exact aspect.
