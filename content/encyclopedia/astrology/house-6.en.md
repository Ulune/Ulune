## Meaning

The 6th house is the house of ordinary days: daily work and its tasks, colleagues and those one works with or for, routines and habits, the care given to the body, the animals one looks after, and the skills that improve with practice. Astrologers read it as the craft of everyday life, the work that keeps things running.

It is a cadent house, falling away from the angle of the 7th, and like the other cadent houses it is read as adjusting and improving. Its opposite is the [[house-12|12th house]], and the axis they form is that of daily order and retreat: the timetable and the quiet hours outside it, service given in plain view and service given unseen.

Tradition gave this house a hard reputation and read it for illness, toil and servitude. Modern astrologers read it less as misfortune than as maintenance: the effort a life asks for, and the habits that make it lighter. A planet here says nothing about future illness; it describes how a person works and looks after themselves.

## In practice

- **[[mars]] in the 6th**, in its joy, is often read as an energetic, practical worker who likes a concrete task; **Venus in the 6th**, as care for a pleasant workplace and good relations with colleagues; **Jupiter in the 6th**, as generous service and a full schedule.
- **The sign on the cusp** describes how routines and tasks are handled, and its ruler shows where daily effort goes.
- **The ruler of the 6th in the 10th** is a common picture of a career built on skill and service.
- **An empty 6th house** means neither weak health nor a weak work ethic; it is read through its cusp and its ruler.

## Across traditions

- **Jyotish** calls this house ripu, the adversary, and reads it for obstacles, rivalry, debts and illness, but also for service and for the strength to overcome them; it is one of the dusthanas, the difficult houses, and also an upachaya, a house said to improve with time and effort.
- **Zi Wei Dou Shu** has a palace of Health among its twelve palaces; it is found by another method and read on its own terms.

## Origin and history

Hellenistic astrologers called this place Bad Fortune, kakē tychē, and made it the joy of Mars; like the 2nd, 8th and 12th, it makes no classical aspect to the rising sign. Lilly read the 6th for servants, for small animals such as sheep, goats and rabbits, for sickness and its causes, and for tenants and uncles. Modern astrology, following the sign of the same number, Virgo, made it above all the house of work, service, health habits and pets.

## How Ulune calculates it

The cusp of the 6th comes from Swiss Ephemeris in the chosen system: in Placidus, Ulune’s default, it is the second time division between the lowest point of the sky and the western horizon; in Porphyry, two thirds of the zodiac arc from the IC to the Descendant; in Equal houses, 150° after the Ascendant; in Whole sign houses, the sixth sign from the rising sign. Its opposite cusp is always that of the 12th. In the table’s balance, the bodies in the 6th count among the cadent houses.
