## Meaning

The Vertex is a point of the sky’s geometry. Draw the great circle that runs from the east point of the horizon through the point overhead to the west point, the prime vertical: it crosses the zodiac twice, and the crossing on the western side is the Vertex, the one in the east the Anti-Vertex. Like the [[ascendant]], it turns with the Earth once a day, so it needs an accurate birth time and place.

It is a modern point, and not every astrologer uses it. Those who do read it as encounters and events that seem to arrive by chance and feel meaningful afterwards, often through other people: a missed train, a stranger’s remark, a meeting that changes a direction. Because it lies in the western half of the chart, near the [[descendant]] side, it is linked with what comes to someone rather than what they set out to do.

The Anti-Vertex, opposite, is read as the person’s own answer to those encounters, the side of the axis that acts.

## In practice

- **A planet within a degree or two of the Vertex** is read as arriving through others: with Venus, a partner met by an unlikely chance; with Saturn, a responsibility that falls into someone’s hands.
- **Its house**, usually the 5th, 6th, 7th or 8th, shows the area where such encounters tend to happen.
- **Between two charts**, one person’s planet on the other’s Vertex is often noted in [[synastry|synastry]] as a meeting that feels destined.
- **Transits and progressions** to the Vertex are watched as times when an outside event can open a new chapter.

## Origin and history

The Vertex comes from twentieth-century American astrology. Edward Johndro worked with the eastern crossing of the prime vertical, which he called the electric Ascendant; Charles Jayne, testing it, found the western point the more responsive, and that point became the Vertex. It belongs to modern Western astrology alone.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune takes the Vertex from the Swiss Ephemeris’s own house calculation for the birth moment and place, the same computation that gives the Ascendant and the Midheaven, and places the Anti-Vertex exactly opposite; no aspect is listed between the two. Without a birth time it is only a noon stand-in and is marked approximate.

In progressions the Vertex advances with the angles, at the Naibod rate in right ascension. It is aspected with the same orbs as the planets, has no motion of its own to show, and the calendar does not use it.
