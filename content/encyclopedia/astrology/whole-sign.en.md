## Meaning

In whole sign houses, a house is a sign. The [[sign]] rising in the east at birth, the one that holds the [[ascendant|Ascendant]], is the whole 1st house, from its first degree to its last. The next sign in the zodiac is the 2nd house, the one after it the 3rd, and so on round the circle. With Scorpio rising, Scorpio is the 1st house, Sagittarius the 2nd, and Leo the 10th.

Precisely: the cusp of the 1st house is 0° of the rising sign, and each following cusp lies exactly 30° further on. Every house is 30° wide, and every house cusp falls at the start of a sign. Nothing about the latitude or the slant of the zodiac changes the houses; only the rising sign does.

This makes the system very simple to read. A planet’s sign and house always go together, and every planet in the same sign shares a house. There are no [[intercepted|intercepted signs]], and the houses never stretch, however far north or south one is born.

The price is that the degrees of the angles float free of the cusps. The Ascendant degree can lie anywhere inside the 1st house: a planet a few degrees above it, but in the same sign, is still in the 1st. The [[midheaven|Midheaven]] usually falls in the 9th, the 10th or the 11th sign. Astrologers who use whole signs read it as a sensitive point of its own, not as a house cusp.

## In practice

- **Houses as places.** Hellenistic astrologers called the houses places, topoi, and read each as a sign seen from the rising sign. The sign opposite is the place of partners; the sign at the bottom of the chart, the place of home and parents.
- **The Midheaven read apart.** Its sign and house show where public life is aimed; with the Midheaven in the 11th sign, for example, career and friendships are often read together.
- **Easy for derived houses.** Counting houses from any planet or from another house, a technique common in traditional and Indian astrology, is natural when every house is a sign.
- **Uncertain birth times.** When the time is only roughly known but the rising sign is clear, whole sign houses stay the same across the whole range.

## Across traditions

- **Jyotish** counts its houses, the bhavas, sign by sign from the rising sign, the lagna, in most of its readings.
- **Hellenistic astrology**, the source of Western horoscopy, used the same counting in its earliest surviving texts.

## Origin and history

The earliest surviving horoscopic texts, such as the Anthology of Vettius Valens in the 2nd century, usually count the places sign by sign from the rising sign. Medieval astrologers in the Islamic world and in Europe came to prefer quadrant houses, and whole sign houses survived mainly in India. They returned to Western astrology from the 1990s, when new translations of Greek and Latin texts showed how central they had been. Robert Hand’s short study of 2000 called them the oldest house system. Chris Brennan’s Hellenistic Astrology (2017) made them a standard choice for astrologers who follow the ancient methods.

## How Ulune calculates it

Choose Whole sign in the birth form’s options. The Swiss Ephemeris computes the cusps with its code W: the 1st cusp at 0° of the sign of the Ascendant, then every 30°. The table shows every house as 30°00′00″ wide, and no sign is ever intercepted. Whole sign houses are computed at every latitude, so the polar fallback never applies to them.

The Ascendant always falls in the 1st house. The Midheaven is placed by the cusps like any other point. Ulune reads it in the house where it actually falls, often the 9th or the 11th, rather than assuming the 10th. In a composite chart in whole signs, the 1st cusp is 0° of the sign of the midpoint Ascendant. Without a birth time, the rising sign comes from a noon stand-in and the houses are marked ~.
