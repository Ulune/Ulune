## Meaning

A kite is a [[grand-trine|grand trine]] with a tail. To three bodies about 120° apart a fourth is added, [[opposition|opposite]] one of them and [[sextile|sextile]] the other two, at the point halfway between them. Drawn on the wheel, the figure looks like a kite: the triangle is its body, the opposition its spine.

The kite answers the grand trine’s weakness. A grand trine flows in a closed circle; the opposition running through the kite brings tension and awareness, and gives the ease of the trines somewhere to go. Many astrologers count it among the most constructive figures a chart can hold: talent with a purpose, and the drive to use it.

The opposition is the place to look first. Its two ends, one corner of the triangle and the fourth body at the tail, show what the figure aims at and what keeps it moving. The two sextiles from the tail tie that tension back into the trine, so that effort and ease feed each other instead of pulling apart.

## In practice

- A grand trine in water joining the [[moon|Moon]], [[mars|Mars]] and [[neptune|Neptune]], with [[saturn|Saturn]] opposite the Moon and sextile the other two: deep sensitivity given structure, an intuition that learns to build.
- The house of the tail shows where the gifts of the trine are put to use.
- A kite still contains its grand trine: read the triangle first, then what the tail asks of it.

## Origin and history

In the *Tetrabiblos* (2nd century), Ptolemy calls the trine and the sextile harmonious and the opposition discordant. The kite holds both kinds in one figure, three trines and two sextiles around a single opposition, and it is read accordingly: as tension that has found its use.

## How Ulune calculates it

- Ulune looks for a grand trine and a fourth body opposite one of its corners and sextile the other two, with the birth chart’s orbs: 8° for the trines and the opposition, 6° for the sextiles, and 6° and 4° for a trine or a sextile to an angle.
- It searches among the ten planets, Chiron, the Ascendant and the Midheaven.
- In the Table’s Patterns part, the corner of the triangle that the fourth body opposes is named as the kite’s focal body. The grand trine inside the kite is listed as well.
