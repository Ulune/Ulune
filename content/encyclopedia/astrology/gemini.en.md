## Meaning

Gemini is the third sign of the zodiac, from 60° to 90°, the last stretch of northern spring. It is [[element-air|air]] in its [[modality-mutable|mutable]] form, ruled by [[mercury]], and astrologers read it as the sign of curiosity and exchange: noticing, naming, asking, connecting.

A planet in Gemini tends to work through words and contact, to gather information from several sources, and to keep more than one option open. Its gifts are quickness, versatility and a light touch; it can see two sides of a question at once, explain one world to another and learn almost anything that can be put into words.

Its difficulties come from the same speed: attention spread thin, depth traded for variety, a restlessness that leaves things half-done. The opposite sign, [[sagittarius]], looks for one meaning behind all the facts; together the pair is read as the axis of information and understanding, of the near and the far.

Tradition counts Gemini among the double-bodied signs, drawn as two figures and standing between two seasons. No planet is exalted here in the traditional scheme, and none falls here; [[jupiter]], whose broad view sits uneasily with Gemini’s detail, is in its [[detriment|detriment]].

## In practice

- **The [[sun]] in Gemini**, from 20 or 21 May to about 21 June (the day shifts from year to year): a sense of self that grows through learning, talking and moving around.
- **The [[moon]] in Gemini**: feelings that need to be put into words; comfort comes from conversation and a little variety.
- **Gemini rising**, on the [[ascendant|Ascendant]]: a lively, curious first impression. Mercury rules the chart, so its sign, house and aspects show where that curiosity goes.
- **Mercury in Gemini** is in its [[domicile|domicile]], quick and many-sided. **Jupiter in Gemini** gathers knowledge widely and has to choose what it believes.
- **Gemini on a house cusp**: that area of life is handled through information, talk and a willingness to try several ways.

## Across traditions

- **Jyotish** calls the sign Mithuna, the couple, and gives it to Mercury; measured from the stars, it begins about 24° further on, and the Sun enters it around 15 June.
- **Human Design** places five gates wholly in Gemini, 20, 16, 35, 45 and 12, with the edges of gates 8 and 15.
- **The Chinese calendar** opens Gemini with Xiǎomǎn, “grain buds”, and sets Mángzhòng, “grain in ear”, at 15° Gemini, where the month of the Horse (wǔ) begins.

## Origin and history

Babylonian astronomers already saw here the Great Twins. The Greeks named them the Didymoi and told the story of Castor and Pollux: when the mortal Castor died, his brother Pollux shared his own immortality with him, and the two now spend their days by turns among the gods and among the dead.

Their two bright stars, Castor and Pollux, mark the Twins’ heads. Through precession they now stand at about 20° and 23° of the sign Cancer. By the boundaries astronomers published in 1930, the Sun crosses the constellation Gemini from about 21 June to 21 July. In the old doctrine of planetary houses, Gemini is Mercury’s day house, and Virgo its night house.

## How Ulune calculates it

A body is in Gemini from 60° to 90° of tropical longitude, measured from the March equinox point. In the dignity table Mercury holds its domicile here; no planet is exalted or in fall, and Jupiter is in detriment. The [[triplicity|triplicity]] rulers are [[saturn]] by day, Mercury by night and Jupiter participating, after Dorotheus; the [[term|terms]] run Mercury to 6°, Jupiter to 12°, [[venus]] to 17°, [[mars]] to 24° and Saturn to 30°, and the [[face|faces]] are Jupiter, Mars and the Sun. The readings’ [[decan|decans]] are those of Gemini, Libra and Aquarius, ruled by Mercury, Venus and Uranus, the modern ruler of Aquarius.
