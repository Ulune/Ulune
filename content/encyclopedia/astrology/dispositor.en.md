## Meaning

Every planet stands in a sign, and every sign has a ruler. That ruler is the planet’s dispositor: Mars disposes of any planet in Aries or Scorpio, Venus of any planet in Taurus or Libra. The image is of a host and a guest. The guest lives in the host’s house and depends, in part, on the host’s means and goodwill.

Tradition reads the dispositor’s condition as colouring the planet it hosts. A planet in a sign whose ruler is strong, well placed and in contact with it is well looked after. A planet whose host is weak, hidden or out of touch must manage more on its own.

The dispositor has a dispositor in turn. Following each ruler to the ruler of its own sign makes a chain. A chain ends when it reaches a planet in its own sign, its [[domicile]], which disposes of itself: a final dispositor. Or it closes in a loop, when planets dispose of one another in a ring.

When every chain in a chart ends at the same planet, that single final dispositor is read as a keynote of the whole chart. Everything else leads back to it. Many charts have no single final dispositor: several end points, or a loop, are just as common.

## In practice

- **A chain that ends.** The Moon in Gemini is disposed by Mercury; Mercury in Taurus by Venus; Venus in Libra is at home. Venus is the final dispositor of this chain.
- **A chain that loops.** The Sun in Sagittarius is disposed by Jupiter; Jupiter in Aries by Mars; Mars in Sagittarius by Jupiter again. Jupiter and Mars dispose of each other: a [[reception|mutual reception]] by domicile.
- **A [[peregrine]] planet** leans on its dispositor more than most, since it has no dignity of its own where it stands.
- **The chart ruler’s dispositor** is often read as the power behind the chart ruler, the planet whose affairs shape how it can act.

## Across traditions

- **Hellenistic astrology** called the ruler of a planet’s sign its domicile lord. It used it constantly, both for judging a planet and for following the thread of a chart.
- **Indian astrology** reads the lord of the sign a planet occupies in the same way, and weighs its strength and house in judging the planet.

## Origin and history

The habit of judging a planet through the ruler of its sign goes back to Hellenistic astrology. Vettius Valens, in the 2nd century, often weighs a placement by the condition of its domicile lord. The English word comes from the Latin *disponere*, to arrange or to manage. William Lilly, in *Christian Astrology* (1647), uses it often for the planet that rules the sign another stands in.

Drawing every chain of rulers into one tree, with a final dispositor at its root, is a habit of modern astrology. With the modern rulers, Uranus, Neptune or Pluto can become final dispositors; with the traditional rulers, only the seven classical planets can.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune follows the dispositors of the ten planets, from the Sun to Pluto. Each planet is disposed by the traditional ruler of its sign, so only the seven classical planets can be dispositors. A final dispositor is a planet in its own traditional sign.

The Dignities table lists the final dispositors, then each other planet’s chain with arrows, ending at a final dispositor or marked as a loop. It names a single final dispositor only when there is exactly one, there is no loop, and every chain ends there. Without a birth time, a chain is marked ~ when one of its planets could change sign during the day, the Moon most often.
