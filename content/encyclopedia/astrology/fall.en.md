## Meaning

A planet is in its fall in the sign opposite its [[exaltation]]. The Sun, exalted in Aries, falls in Libra. The Moon falls in Scorpio, Mercury in Pisces, Venus in Virgo, Mars in Cancer, Jupiter in Capricorn and Saturn in Aries.

Where exaltation raises a planet like an honoured guest, fall lowers it. Tradition reads a planet in fall as ill at ease and little esteemed: its qualities are present but undervalued, or expressed with hesitation. It is a debility, one step lighter than [[detriment]]. William Lilly gives it minus four points against minus five for detriment.

The difference is one of tone. In detriment a planet is far from home, in a house run by a contrary ruler. In fall it is simply not honoured: it has to earn the regard that elsewhere comes to it freely.

Modern readings often see fall as a place of humility. A planet that cannot count on admiration may learn patience, empathy and a quieter kind of strength.

## In practice

- **The Sun in Libra**: a sense of self shaped through others, through fairness and partnership, sometimes at the cost of one’s own direction.
- **The Moon in Scorpio**: feelings deep, intense and guarded, slow to trust and loyal once given.
- **Venus in Virgo**: love shown through practical care and attention to detail, with a critical eye that can turn inward.
- **Mars in Cancer**: drive tied to feelings and to the protection of one’s own, often indirect, strong when defending others.
- **Jupiter in Capricorn**: faith tested by realism, growth earned through structure and effort.
- **Saturn in Aries**: impatience with limits; authority learned by acting and correcting course.
- **Mercury in Pisces** is both in detriment and in fall: a mind that works in images, moods and intuitions more than in lists.

## Across traditions

- **Indian astrology** calls fall *nīcha*, debilitation, in the same signs. The deepest debilitation lies at the degree opposite the deepest exaltation, such as the Sun at 10° Libra.
- **It also knows a cancellation of debilitation**, *nīcha bhaṅga*, when other factors in the chart support the planet. Western tradition, too, weighs fall against the rest of the chart.

## Origin and history

Greek astrologers called this place a planet’s *tapeinōma*, its lowering, the opposite of its *hypsōma*, its height. The Latin *casus* and the French *chute* both mean a fall.

Ptolemy gives the reason in the *Tetrabiblos* for the Sun. In Libra it begins to sink into the southern half of the sky, and the days shorten. Its exaltation in Aries has its counterpart there. The same reasoning by opposition gives each planet its fall.

Lilly, in *Christian Astrology* (1647), scores fall at minus four, the counterweight of the four points of exaltation. He judged a planet as a whole, and a planet in fall could still be helped by its house, its aspects or a reception.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune counts fall by the whole sign, for the seven traditional planets. A planet in the sign opposite its exaltation loses four of Lilly’s points, and any dignity it holds there is added. Mercury in Pisces is in detriment and in fall at once: −5 − 4 = −9 before any term or face.

The Dignities table names, for each degree, the planet in fall there, or none in Taurus, Gemini, Leo, Sagittarius and Aquarius. The wheel’s dignity overlay shows one mark per planet. For Mercury in Pisces it shows the detriment, while the table lists both. A planet in fall is never also called [[peregrine]] in Ulune. Uranus, Neptune and Pluto have no fall.
