## Meaning

A quintile joins two bodies a fifth of the circle apart, 72°. Unlike the Ptolemaic aspects it does not fit the twelve signs: a fifth of the zodiac is two signs and twelve degrees, so the quintile follows no relation of element or modality and can fall between signs two or three apart.

Many modern astrologers read the fifth division of the circle as the number of form, style and skill. The quintile is linked with talent, play and the wish to make something one’s own: not effortless like a [[trine|trine]], more like a knack that grows with practice until it becomes a signature.

As a fine division, it is used with a narrow orb and read as a nuance. It adds a creative turn to the two functions it joins, and is noticed mostly when it is close to exact. Its companion, the biquintile at 144°, two fifths of the circle, is read in the same family.

## In practice

- [[mercury|Mercury]] quintile [[uranus|Uranus]]: an inventive turn of mind, ideas with a personal stamp.
- [[venus|Venus]] quintile [[mars|Mars]]: flair in the way charm and desire are expressed, often through art, dance or design.
- A quintile within a degree deserves a look even in a busy chart; a wide one adds little.

## Origin and history

The quintile is one of Johannes Kepler’s new aspects. He first suggested that planets 72°, 144° or 135° apart might matter in 1596, and in *De Fundamentis Astrologiae Certioribus* (1601) he set out the quintile, the biquintile and the sesquiquadrate as aspects in their own right, grounding them in musical harmony and, he said, in years of observing the weather (David Juste, 2010). The name comes from the Latin *quintus*, fifth.

## How Ulune calculates it

- The quintile is a minor aspect in Ulune, with an orb of 2°, to an angle as well, and 1°30′ in synastry.
- A fifth of the circle has no matching count of signs, so a quintile is never marked out of sign.
- Ulune computes the quintile but not the biquintile, and does not use it in the configurations.
