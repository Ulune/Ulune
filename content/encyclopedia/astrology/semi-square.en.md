## Meaning

A semi-square joins two bodies 45° apart, an eighth of the circle, half a [[square|square]]. It belongs to the square’s family and carries a lighter version of its friction: less a crisis than an itch, a recurring irritation between two functions that never quite settle together.

Forty-five degrees is a sign and a half, so the semi-square follows no rule of signs. Depending on where each body stands in its sign, the two signs are one or two apart, and never share an element or a modality.

It is usually read as a spur. A semi-square seldom dominates a chart, but it keeps a matter from going quiet: small frictions that, once noticed as a pattern, point to something worth adjusting. Its companion at three eighths of the circle, the sesquiquadrate (135°), is read in the same way by those who use it.

## In practice

- The [[sun|Sun]] semi-square [[mars|Mars]]: restlessness and impatience that, given a direction, turn into energy for work.
- [[venus|Venus]] semi-square [[saturn|Saturn]]: affection and caution rub against each other, and closeness comes with small hesitations that patience eases.
- When the same planet also makes squares or oppositions, a semi-square adds to their theme in a lower voice: read it alongside them.

## Across traditions

- **Cosmobiology**, which Reinhold Ebertin built on the 90° dial of the Hamburg School (*Kombination der Gestirneinflüsse*, 1940), works mainly with the hard aspects, the multiples of 45°. There the semi-square and the sesquiquadrate belong to one family with the conjunction, the square and the opposition.
- **Ptolemy’s** scheme has no place for it: his configurations join whole signs, and 45° is not a whole number of signs.

## How Ulune calculates it

- The semi-square is a minor aspect in Ulune, with an orb of 2°30′, to an angle as well, and 1°30′ in synastry.
- Since it has no fixed count of signs, it is never marked out of sign.
- It is not used in the configurations, and Ulune does not compute the sesquiquadrate.
