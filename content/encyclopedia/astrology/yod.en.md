## Meaning

A yod forms when two bodies in [[sextile|sextile]] are both [[quincunx|quincunx]] a third: a tall, narrow triangle of 60° and twice 150°. The third body is its apex. The figure is named after the Hebrew letter yod, and is also called the finger of God, names that sound far more fateful than most modern readings of it.

The yod is built of quincunxes and carries their quality: no open conflict, but a lasting mismatch that asks for adjustment. The two planets at its base cooperate through their sextile, and both make demands on the apex that it cannot meet in the usual way. The apex planet becomes the place where a person keeps fine-tuning, experimenting, looking for the right form.

Many astrologers read in a yod the sense of a particular task, something to be found rather than given. Read calmly, it describes a skill that grows through adjustment: at the apex, steady small corrections become a way of working no one else quite has.

The point opposite the apex lies halfway between the two planets of the base, and some astrologers read it as the place where the yod’s tension finds release.

## In practice

- [[venus|Venus]] sextile [[jupiter|Jupiter]], both quincunx [[saturn|Saturn]]: the ease of the base meets Saturn’s sense of duty at the apex, and the task is a form of commitment that does not cost one’s joy.
- The apex planet’s house shows where the adjustments are made.
- Yods are easy to overrate: they rest on minor aspects with narrow orbs, and a wide one adds little.

## Origin and history

Classical astrology could not know this figure: in the *Tetrabiblos* (2nd century), Ptolemy calls signs five apart inconjunct, so the quincunx, the yod’s main aspect, was no aspect at all. The yod belongs to modern astrology, which counts the quincunx among its aspects.

## How Ulune calculates it

- Ulune looks for two bodies in sextile and a third quincunx to both, with the birth chart’s orbs: 6° for the sextile (4° when one end is an angle) and 3° for each quincunx.
- The quincunx is a minor aspect in Ulune, but it counts here.
- It searches among the ten planets, Chiron, the Ascendant and the Midheaven, and names the apex as the focal body in the Table’s Patterns part.
- Without a birth time, a figure that may not hold all day is marked ~.
