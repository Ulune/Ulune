## Meaning

Gate 2 is the gate of the Direction of the Self. It carries an inner sense of where things should go. Human Design reads it as a gate that steers rather than produces: it knows which way a project or a household should head, often without being able to explain why, and others end up following that quiet certainty.

It sits in the [[hd-centre-g|G centre]], the centre of identity, direction and love in the bodygraph. Where [[hd-gate-1]] gives the self a creative voice, gate 2 gives it a heading. The tradition places it in the [[hd-circuit-knowing|Knowing circuit]], part of the [[hd-circuit-individual|Individual circuit group]], which Human Design associates with mutation and with knowing that arrives whole, before any proof.

The gate’s gift is receptive. It does not push the way forward by force; it orients the resources that others bring. The tradition reads a sense of drifting, when it comes, as a sign that the direction has not yet been found, not as a fault.

## In practice

- **Personality**, in ink: a planet stood in gate 2 at birth. Human Design reads it as the side of the theme a person knows in themselves.
- **Design**, in red: a planet stood here about three months before birth, when the Sun was 88° earlier. It is the side others often see more clearly.
- **Alone, as a [[hd-hanging|hanging gate]]**: gate 2 is coloured but its partner, [[hd-gate-14]], is not. The direction is there, but the energy to fund it comes from outside: someone who carries gate 14 can complete the channel while they are together.
- **Completed**: with gate 14 coloured too, the [[hd-channel-2-14|channel of the Beat]] is defined, joining the G centre to the [[hd-centre-sacral|Sacral]]. Ulune’s texts note it is often called the Keeper of Keys: a steady sense of direction joined to the energy that generates resources.

When the Sun stands in gate 2, the Earth stands exactly opposite in gate 1, so the two gates often appear together as a pair of Sun and Earth.

## Across traditions

- **The I Ching**: hexagram 2, Kūn 坤, which Wilhelm called The Receptive. It is the only hexagram of six broken lines, the trigram of Earth doubled, the mirror of hexagram 1. Legge’s judgment ends on “the firmness of a mare” (Legge, 1882), an image of strength that follows rather than leads.
- **Astrology**: the gate covers the middle of [[taurus|Taurus]], a sign astrologers read as steadiness and the value of things. The Sun crosses it each year roughly from 3 to 9 May.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune lays the 64 [[hd-gate|gates]] around the tropical zodiac from gate 41 at 302°, 2°00′00″ Aquarius, each 5.625° or 5°37′30″ wide, in the wheel’s order. Gate 2 is the 19th, between gates 24 and 23, so it begins 18 × 5.625° = 101.25° after gate 41. It runs from 13°15′00″ to 18°52′30″ Taurus, that is from 43.25° to 48.875°.

Its six [[hd-line|lines]] are 0°56′15″ each. Line 1 starts at 13°15′00″, line 2 at 14°11′15″, line 3 at 15°07′30″, line 4 at 16°03′45″, line 5 at 17°00′00″ and line 6 at 17°56′15″ Taurus.

Any of the thirteen bodies Ulune places at birth and at the Design moment that falls in this span colours gate 2, with its line. Without a birth time, a gate or line that could differ at another hour of the day is marked.
