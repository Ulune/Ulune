## Meaning

Line 4 is the bottom of the upper trigram, the first place of the hexagram’s outer half. Human Design calls it the Opportunist. The word is not meant unkindly: its opportunities simply come through people it already knows.

Where the fourth line is active, the tradition describes a life that runs through relationships. Work, ideas and chances travel along a network of friends, colleagues and family. Influence works best close to home, on people who already trust the person, rather than on strangers.

Human Design reads the upper trigram, lines 4 to 6, as the transpersonal half of the hexagram, turned towards others. The fourth line is its threshold. What the first line built inside, the fourth carries out into a circle of people.

Because so much rests on that circle, stability matters to this line. Ulune notes that someone with a fourth line may feel unsettled when a long friendship ends without a clear replacement. The tradition reads that unease as a sign of what the line values, not as weakness.

## In practice

- **In a gate.** A planet on the fourth line gives the [[hd-gate|gate]]’s theme a social, relational shade. The gate is lived and shared through friendship. It is written after the gate number: 34.4 is gate 34, line 4.
- **In a profile.** When the Personality Sun or the Design Sun stands on line 4, it becomes half of the [[hd-profile|profile]]. Four profiles hold it: [[hd-profile-1-4|1/4]], [[hd-profile-2-4|2/4]], [[hd-profile-4-6|4/6]] and [[hd-profile-4-1|4/1]].
- **Conscious or not.** As the first number (4/6, 4/1), the reliance on a network is known and chosen. As the second number (1/4, 2/4), it works in the [[hd-layers|Design]], and chances arrive through friends almost without being sought.
- **An everyday picture.** Ulune’s own example: someone who gets most of their jobs by recommendation.
- **The harmonic.** Line 4 pairs with [[hd-line-1|line 1]], the bottom of the lower trigram. Both are foundations, inner for the first, shared for the fourth. The 1/4 and 4/1 profiles join the pair; 4/1 is also the only profile with a [[hd-cross-juxtaposition|Juxtaposition cross]].

## Origin and history

The six places come from the I Ching, the Chinese Book of Changes, whose 64 hexagrams Human Design takes as its gates. Ra Uru Hu, who founded the system in 1987, gave each place a name and a theme of his own.

Classical reading made the fourth place that of the minister, close to the ruler in the fifth. Nearness brings both access and care. The Great Appendix, in Legge’s translation of 1882, calls the fourth line “the subject of many apprehensions”, from its closeness to the fifth.

In the first hexagram, The Creative, Wilhelm’s fourth line reads “Wavering flight over the depths. No blame.” It is a moment between levels, leaving the lower trigram for the upper. Human Design’s fourth line also stands at a threshold, between the personal and the shared.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune reads the line from the ecliptic longitude of each planet and each Node, at birth and at the Design moment. Its wheel starts with gate 41 at 302°, or 2° Aquarius. Each gate spans 5.625°, each line 0.9375°, or 0°56′15″.

Ulune subtracts 302° from the longitude and wraps the result into 0–360°. Divided by 5.625° and rounded down, it gives the gate’s place in the wheel. The remainder, divided by 0.9375° and rounded down, plus one, gives the line.

Line 4 is therefore the fourth 0.9375° of every gate, from 2.8125° to 3.75° into it, counted in the direction of the zodiac. In gate 41 it runs from 4°48′45″ to 5°45′00″ Aquarius. A longitude exactly on a boundary starts the next line.

The finer count used for the [[hd-variable|arrows]] always agrees with this line. The Earth, exactly 192 lines from the Sun, always shares its line. Without a birth time, the chart is cast at noon and every hour of the day. A line that changes in that span is marked ~.
