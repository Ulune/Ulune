## Meaning

The Throat sits in the upper part of the bodygraph, drawn as a square below the [[hd-centre-ajna|Ajna]]. It is the centre of communication and action. Everything that is said or done passes through it. Human Design calls it the centre of manifestation: the only one that turns energy into something others can hear or see.

That role explains its shape on the chart. Eleven gates sit on its rim, and thirteen channels reach it, more than any other centre. It is joined to every centre except the [[hd-centre-head|Head]] and the [[hd-centre-root|Root]]. The tradition says that every other centre, in one way or another, is trying to reach the Throat.

The Throat is not a motor, not a pressure centre and not an awareness centre. It expresses what the others bring. What reaches it decides how it speaks. The Ajna brings ideas, the [[hd-centre-g|G centre]] a sense of self, the [[hd-centre-solar-plexus|Solar Plexus]] feelings. The [[hd-centre-spleen|Spleen]] brings instinct, the [[hd-centre-heart|Heart]] will, the [[hd-centre-sacral|Sacral]] life force.

Defined, the Throat gives a consistent way of speaking and acting. The voice and style stay recognisable in any company. The centres linked to it show what that voice carries.

Undefined or open, the Throat lets one’s way of speaking change with the people around. There can be a pressure to talk, to be noticed or to fill a silence. Human Design reads this kindly, as a signal rather than a fault. Words tend to land best when someone has asked for them, or when the moment clearly calls for them.

## In practice

- **Defined**: one of its thirteen channels is complete. The question is then what it connects to.
- **A motor joined to the Throat**: through one channel or a chain of defined centres, a motor reaching the Throat can act on its own. Without a defined Sacral this makes a [[hd-manifestor|Manifestor]]; with one, a [[hd-manifesting-generator|Manifesting Generator]].
- **No motor joined**: a defined Throat linked only to the Ajna, the G or the Spleen points to a [[hd-projector|Projector]]. With a defined Sacral elsewhere, it is a [[hd-generator|Generator]].
- **Undefined, with active gates**: each [[hd-hanging|hanging gate]] here is one way of speaking that others can complete.
- **Authority**: the G joined to the Throat gives [[hd-authority-self-projected|self-projected authority]]. The Heart joined to it is one form of [[hd-authority-ego|ego authority]]. A chart defined only in the Head, the Ajna and the Throat has [[hd-authority-mental|mental authority]].

## Across traditions

- **The chakras**: Human Design says the Throat derives from Vishuddha, the throat chakra of yoga, whose name is usually read as “pure”. In the yoga texts it governs speech and sound. Of all the centres, the Throat stays closest to its chakra’s meaning.
- **A crossroads**: in many traditions the voice is the bridge between inner life and the world. Human Design takes the idea literally and draws the Throat as the hub where most channels meet.

## Origin and history

Ra Uru Hu taught the nine-centred bodygraph from 1987, presenting it as a change from the seven chakras that he dated to 1781. The tradition links the Throat to the thyroid and parathyroid glands, and through them to metabolism. That is a correspondence the system teaches, not a medical claim.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune colours a gate when any of the 26 activations stands in it, the 13 bodies of the Personality and the 13 of the Design. A channel is defined when both of its gates are coloured, and a centre is defined when a defined channel touches it.

The Throat is defined exactly when one of its thirteen channels is defined. Otherwise it is undefined when any of its eleven gates is coloured, open when none is. For type, Ulune walks from the Throat along every defined channel. If that walk reaches the Sacral, the Solar Plexus, the Heart or the Root, a motor is joined to the Throat. For authority, a defined G and a defined Throat give self-projected authority, once the Solar Plexus, Sacral, Spleen and Heart are all undefined.
