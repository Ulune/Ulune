## Meaning

The Root is the square at the bottom of the bodygraph. Human Design gives it two roles. It is a pressure centre, like the [[hd-centre-head|Head]] at the top: it pushes rather than acts. It is also a motor, with the [[hd-centre-sacral|Sacral]], the [[hd-centre-solar-plexus|Solar Plexus]] and the [[hd-centre-heart|Heart]].

Its pressure is the adrenaline and stress that get life moving. It sets the pace: deadlines, urgency, the push to begin something or to see it through. Where the Head presses to understand, the Root presses to do. The tradition does not treat this stress as bad. It is fuel, and the question is how it is handled.

Its nine gates send that pressure up in three directions. Three feed the [[hd-centre-spleen|Spleen]]: [[hd-gate-54|gate 54]] ambition, [[hd-gate-38|gate 38]] the fighter’s stubbornness, [[hd-gate-58|gate 58]] vitality and the joy of improving things. Three feed the Sacral: [[hd-gate-53|gate 53]] beginnings, [[hd-gate-60|gate 60]] acceptance of limits, [[hd-gate-52|gate 52]] stillness and focus. Three feed the Solar Plexus: [[hd-gate-19|gate 19]] the wish for closeness, [[hd-gate-39|gate 39]] provocation, [[hd-gate-41|gate 41]] contraction, the pressure to have new experiences.

Defined, the Root handles pressure in its own consistent way and uses it as fuel. Such a person tends to work at their own pace. The tradition notes that they may put pressure on others without meaning to.

Undefined or open, the Root takes in the pressure around it and amplifies it. One may rush to finish things just to be rid of the stress. Human Design reads this as sensitivity, not weakness, and offers a calm reminder: not every deadline is urgent, and not every deadline is one’s own. Answering every message the moment it arrives is a typical sign; letting some wait is a good practice.

## In practice

- **Defined**: one of nine channels is complete, to the Spleen, the Sacral or the Solar Plexus. The Root has no channel to the [[hd-centre-throat|Throat]].
- **Type**: as a motor, the Root can make a [[hd-manifestor|Manifestor]], but only through another centre. A defined Spleen joined to the Throat, for instance, can carry its pressure there.
- **Authority**: the Root is never an [[hd-authority|authority]]. Its pressure can make choices feel urgent, which the tradition treats as a reason to slow down and use the chart’s own authority.
- **Undefined, with active gates**: a [[hd-hanging|hanging gate]] here names the kind of pressure most easily picked up from others.
- **Open**: no gate is coloured. Calm surroundings matter, because the Root mirrors the stress of the place it is in.

## Across traditions

- **The chakras**: Human Design says the Root derives from Muladhara, the root chakra of yoga at the base of the spine. In the yoga texts, Muladhara is the seat of earth and of the coiled energy called kundalini. Both share the themes of survival and grounding; the Root adds pressure and pace.
- **Above and below**: the bodygraph is built between its two pressure centres. The Head presses down towards thought, the Root presses up towards action.

## Origin and history

Ra Uru Hu presented the nine centres from 1987, as a change from the seven chakras that he dated to 1781. The root chakra kept its place at the base of the body and became the Root centre. The tradition links it to the adrenal glands, the source of adrenaline. That is a correspondence the system teaches, never a medical claim or advice.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune colours a gate when any of the 26 activations stands in it, the 13 bodies of the Personality and the 13 of the Design. A channel is defined when both of its gates are coloured, and a centre is defined when a defined channel touches it.

The Root is defined exactly when one of its nine channels is defined. Otherwise it is undefined when any of its nine gates is coloured, open when none is. Ulune counts it among the four motors. It walks from the Throat along defined channels. A Root reached only through a defined Spleen still counts as a motor joined to the Throat. Ulune’s authority test never looks at the Root.
