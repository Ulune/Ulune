## Meaning

Human Design gives each [[hd-type|type]] two feelings to watch for. The signature is the feeling of being on track: life flows, effort pays, people respond. The not-self theme is the feeling that says something is pulling against the grain. They are signposts, not verdicts, and everyone feels both at times.

| Type | Signature | Not-self theme |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[hd-manifestor|Manifestor]] | Peace | Anger |
| [[hd-generator|Generator]] | Satisfaction | Frustration |
| [[hd-manifesting-generator|Manifesting Generator]] | Satisfaction | Frustration and anger |
| [[hd-projector|Projector]] | Success | Bitterness |
| [[hd-reflector|Reflector]] | Surprise | Disappointment |

The word “not-self” comes from a central idea of the system. Human Design teaches that the [[hd-undefined-open|undefined and open centres]], the white ones, take in the energy of the people around and amplify it. Over a lifetime this conditioning can lead a person to live by borrowed pressures. One hurries because others hurry, or decides from the mind because a decision seems expected. The tradition calls that borrowed way of living the not-self.

Its usual remedy is the pair the tradition puts at the centre: [[hd-strategy|strategy]] and [[hd-authority|authority]]. When a person follows them, Human Design teaches, the signature tends to grow and the not-self theme to fade.

Each pair matches its type’s way of working. A Generator’s energy is made to be used on what it responds to, so satisfaction marks success and frustration marks energy spent elsewhere. A Manifestor needs room to act, so peace marks freedom and anger marks being held back. A Projector needs to be seen, so success means recognition and bitterness its lack. A Reflector needs the right surroundings, so surprise marks a life that keeps delighting and disappointment one that does not.

These feelings are read kindly. Anger, frustration, bitterness or disappointment are not faults of character. They are useful information: an invitation to look at what led there, and gently to change course.

## In practice

- **Noticing the theme** is the first step. A Projector who feels bitter after a long meeting might ask whether their advice had been invited.
- **Tracing it back** often helps. A Generator’s frustration may lead back to a commitment the gut never agreed to; a Manifestor’s anger to a plan others were not told about.
- **Watching the signature** matters as much. Moments of peace, satisfaction, success or surprise show what to keep doing.
- **Not every bad day** is a not-self signal. Frustration, for a Generator, can also simply be part of learning a skill.

Many people keep a short log for a while, noting when each feeling appears and what came just before.

## Origin and history

The signature and the not-self theme belong to the system Ra Uru Hu said he received on Ibiza in 1987. He set it out with Lynda Bunnell in *The Definitive Book of Human Design* (2011). Ra Uru Hu used the idea of the not-self throughout his teaching, to describe life lived under conditioning rather than by one’s own design. Chetan Parkyn’s *Human Design* (2009) presents the same pairs for general readers.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune reads the pair straight from the type. The Keys table under the bodygraph shows it on a line headed “Signature · not-self”, for example “Satisfaction · Frustration” for a Generator. For a Manifesting Generator it shows “Satisfaction · Frustration and anger”. Without a birth time, the line is marked ~ whenever the type itself could differ at another hour of that day.
