## Meaning

Line 5 is the middle of the upper trigram, the second place from the top. Human Design calls it the Heretic: someone who brings an unexpected, practical answer from outside the usual frame.

Its central theme is projection. Other people, often people who barely know this person, see in them the one who can solve their problem. Human Design calls this a projection field. It opens doors quickly, because others come with trust already given.

The field works in both directions. When the fifth line delivers a solution that helps, its reputation grows. When the expected help does not come, the same projection can turn, and others may feel let down. Ulune’s texts describe both sides plainly.

The tradition does not read this as a trap, but as a reason for care. It means choosing which calls to answer, and offering solutions sound enough to work for many. A firm base of knowledge, often from [[hd-line-1|line 1]] or [[hd-line-2|line 2]], helps the fifth line keep its reputation steady.

## In practice

- **In a gate.** A planet on the fifth line gives the [[hd-gate|gate]]’s theme a public, problem-solving shade. Others tend to look to that gate for help. It is written after the gate number: 34.5 is gate 34, line 5.
- **In a profile.** When the Personality Sun or the Design Sun stands on line 5, it becomes half of the [[hd-profile|profile]]. Four profiles hold it: [[hd-profile-2-5|2/5]], [[hd-profile-3-5|3/5]], [[hd-profile-5-1|5/1]] and [[hd-profile-5-2|5/2]].
- **Conscious or not.** As the first number (5/1, 5/2), the projection is felt and recognised. As the second number (2/5, 3/5), it works in the [[hd-layers|Design]]: strangers call on the person even when they would rather stay unseen.
- **An everyday picture.** Ulune’s own example: a new colleague who is expected to fix everything in their first month.
- **The harmonic.** Line 5 pairs with line 2, the middle of the lower trigram. Both are seen by others: the fifth is looked to for solutions, the second is called out for its natural gift. The 2/5 and 5/2 profiles join the pair.

## Origin and history

The six places come from the I Ching, the Chinese Book of Changes, whose 64 hexagrams Human Design takes as its gates. Ra Uru Hu, who founded the system in 1987, gave each place a name and a theme of his own.

In classical reading the fifth place is the most honoured: it is the place of the ruler, central in the upper trigram. The Great Appendix, in Legge’s translation of 1882, says the occupant of the fifth place “achieves much merit”, since it holds the noble position.

In the first hexagram, Wilhelm’s fifth line reads “Flying dragon in the heavens.” The strength is fully visible and others look up to it. Human Design’s fifth line inherits that visibility, together with the expectations it brings.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune reads the line from the ecliptic longitude of each planet and each Node, at birth and at the Design moment. Its wheel starts with gate 41 at 302°, or 2° Aquarius. Each gate spans 5.625°, each line 0.9375°, or 0°56′15″.

Ulune subtracts 302° from the longitude and wraps the result into 0–360°. Divided by 5.625° and rounded down, it gives the gate’s place in the wheel. The remainder, divided by 0.9375° and rounded down, plus one, gives the line.

Line 5 is therefore the fifth 0.9375° of every gate, from 3.75° to 4.6875° into it, counted in the direction of the zodiac. In gate 41 it runs from 5°45′00″ to 6°41′15″ Aquarius. A longitude exactly on a boundary starts the next line.

The finer count used for the [[hd-variable|arrows]] always agrees with this line. The Earth, exactly 192 lines from the Sun, always shares its line. Without a birth time, the chart is cast at noon and every hour of the day. A line that changes in that span is marked ~.
