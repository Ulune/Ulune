## Meaning

Human Design sorts its 36 [[hd-channel|channels]] into circuits, families of channels that carry the same kind of energy. It gathers the circuits into three groups, each a different way of sharing energy. The Collective circuitry is the largest, with fourteen channels. Its keynote is sharing.

The tradition contrasts it with the two other groups. The [[hd-circuit-individual|Individual circuitry]] brings something new and touches others by example. The [[hd-circuit-tribal|Tribal circuitry]] shares within a known circle, through bonds and bargains. Collective energy reaches beyond any circle. It is meant for humanity as a whole, strangers included, and it is impersonal in a generous sense. What it learns is not kept: it wants to be passed on.

Human Design describes the Collective as looking at life through patterns and experiences, then offering what it finds. Its gifts are often public. Teaching, storytelling, research, leadership and the arts are common examples in the tradition’s teaching. Six of its fourteen channels reach the [[hd-centre-throat|Throat]], the centre of speech and action, which suits a group made to share.

The group holds two circuits, which mirror each other:

- The [[hd-circuit-logic|Logic circuit]], also called the Understanding circuit, looks ahead. It finds a pattern, tests it, corrects it and offers a safer future. Its seven channels move from doubt to formula to detailed opinion.
- The [[hd-circuit-sensing|Sensing circuit]], also called the Abstract circuit, looks back. It lives an experience fully, then reflects on it and tells what it meant. Its seven channels move from desire to experience to story.

Between them, the tradition sees the two halves of human learning: the pattern that can be repeated, and the experience that can be remembered.

## In practice

- **Reading a chart.** Ulune draws every defined channel on the bodygraph but does not group the channels by circuit. To find the Collective ones, compare the defined channels with the lists on the Logic and Sensing pages.
- **Sharing as a need.** A chart rich in Collective channels is read as needing an audience for what it understands. The tradition notes that unshared insight can feel stuck, and that sharing works best when it is asked for.
- **Invitation and recognition.** Collective gifts are for others, so the tradition places weight on being recognised. Advice offered to someone who did not ask is often not heard, however sound it is.
- **Impersonal does not mean cold.** Human Design reads the Collective’s distance as a kind of fairness: it serves everyone, not only its own people. A Tribal partner may still feel it as aloof, and naming that difference helps.
- **Future and past.** Logic and Sensing can clash in a room, one wanting proof, the other wanting to try. The tradition reads them as partners, each correcting the other’s blind spot.

## Across traditions

- **The I Ching.** The last two hexagrams of the I Ching sit at the heads of the two Collective circuits. Wilhelm calls 63 After Completion, in the Logic circuit, and 64 Before Completion, in the Sensing circuit. The match is striking, though Human Design did not group its gates by the book’s order.
- **Astrology.** Modern astrology calls the outer planets generational, because they move slowly and mark whole age groups. The likeness with the Collective is loose, but both describe what is shared beyond one’s own circle. See [[neptune]].

## Origin and history

The Collective circuitry belongs to Ra Uru Hu’s teaching on circuits, presented from 1987. The Definitive Book of Human Design (Ra Uru Hu and Lynda Bunnell, 2011) describes it. Its two circuits go by two names each in today’s teaching: Logic or Understanding, Sensing or Abstract.
