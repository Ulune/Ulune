## Meaning

Line 6 is the top of the hexagram, the last of the six places. Human Design calls it the Role Model. Its theme is perspective: the view from the end of a process, where the whole can be seen at once.

Human Design describes this line as unfolding in three phases. Until about 30, it experiments much like [[hd-line-3|line 3]], learning through what works and what does not. From about 30 to about 50, it steps back and observes, often more reserved than before. After about 50, it embodies what it learned, and others come to it for grounded advice.

The tradition reads these phases as a natural rhythm, not a schedule to keep. The middle stretch can look like withdrawal. Human Design sees it as digestion, where experiments turn into understanding.

The sixth line often sets high standards, for itself and for others. The tradition reads that hope as a strength. By living its values, the line becomes an example without needing to preach.

## In practice

- **In a gate.** A planet on the sixth line gives the [[hd-gate|gate]]’s theme a wider, overseeing shade. The gate is lived, then watched, then embodied. It is written after the gate number: 34.6 is gate 34, line 6.
- **In a profile.** When the Personality Sun or the Design Sun stands on line 6, it becomes half of the [[hd-profile|profile]]. Four profiles hold it: [[hd-profile-3-6|3/6]], [[hd-profile-4-6|4/6]], [[hd-profile-6-2|6/2]] and [[hd-profile-6-3|6/3]].
- **Conscious or not.** As the first number (6/2, 6/3), the search for perspective is felt and known. As the second number (3/6, 4/6), the three phases run in the [[hd-layers|Design]], and others may notice them first.
- **An everyday picture.** Ulune’s own example: after an eventful youth and a quieter middle stretch, someone becomes the person others ask for grounded advice.
- **The harmonic.** Line 6 pairs with line 3, the top of the lower trigram. The sixth line’s first phase lives much like a third. The 3/6 and 6/3 profiles join the pair.

## Origin and history

The six places come from the I Ching, the Chinese Book of Changes, whose 64 hexagrams Human Design takes as its gates. Ra Uru Hu, who founded the system in 1987, gave each place a name and a theme of his own.

In classical reading the top place stands at the end of the situation, often a little beyond it. The Great Appendix, in Legge’s translation of 1882, finds the topmost line easy to know and the first one hard. They form the beginning and the end. Wilhelm heads this line “at the top”.

In the first hexagram, Wilhelm’s top line reads “Arrogant dragon will have cause to repent.” Strength that climbs past its moment loses touch. Human Design’s sixth line takes a gentler path from the same height: it steps back, watches, and comes down again as an example.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune reads the line from the ecliptic longitude of each planet and each Node, at birth and at the Design moment. Its wheel starts with gate 41 at 302°, or 2° Aquarius. Each gate spans 5.625°, each line 0.9375°, or 0°56′15″.

Ulune subtracts 302° from the longitude and wraps the result into 0–360°. Divided by 5.625° and rounded down, it gives the gate’s place in the wheel. The remainder, divided by 0.9375° and rounded down, plus one, gives the line.

Line 6 is therefore the last 0.9375° of every gate, from 4.6875° to its end at 5.625°, counted in the direction of the zodiac. In gate 41 it runs from 6°41′15″ to 7°37′30″ Aquarius, where gate 19 begins. A longitude exactly on a boundary starts the next line.

The finer count used for the [[hd-variable|arrows]] always agrees with this line. The Earth, exactly 192 lines from the Sun, always shares its line. Without a birth time, the chart is cast at noon and every hour of the day. A line that changes in that span is marked ~. Ulune does not compute the ages of the three phases: they are a teaching, not a calculation.
