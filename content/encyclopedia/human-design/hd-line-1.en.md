## Meaning

Line 1 is the bottom of the hexagram, the first of the six places in every [[hd-gate|gate]]. Human Design calls it the Investigator and reads it as the line of foundations. Its theme is the ground under one’s feet: knowing how something works before trusting it.

Where the first line is active, security comes from understanding. The tradition describes a mind that researches, reads, checks and wants the facts in place. Acting on thin information tends to feel unsteady. Once the ground has been studied, confidence follows of its own accord.

Line 1 is also the base of the lower trigram, lines 1 to 3. Human Design reads that lower half as the personal, inward-facing part of the hexagram. The first line studies for its own sake first; what it builds can be shared later, often through [[hd-line-4|line 4]].

The unease of acting without enough knowledge is read as a signal, not a flaw. It points to the line’s own remedy: a little more study, a clearer base, then movement.

## In practice

- **In a gate.** A planet on the first line gives the gate’s theme a studious, foundational shade. The gate is approached through understanding before expression. It is written after the gate number: 34.1 is gate 34, line 1.
- **In a profile.** When the Personality Sun or the Design Sun stands on line 1, it becomes half of the [[hd-profile|profile]]. Four profiles hold it: [[hd-profile-1-3|1/3]], [[hd-profile-1-4|1/4]], [[hd-profile-4-1|4/1]] and [[hd-profile-5-1|5/1]].
- **Conscious or not.** As the first number (1/3, 1/4), the line belongs to the [[hd-layers|Personality]], the side a person recognises in themselves. As the second number (4/1, 5/1), it works in the Design, often more visible to others.
- **An everyday picture.** Ulune’s own example: someone who prepares thoroughly before a job interview or a move.
- **The harmonic.** Line 1 pairs with line 4, the first line of the upper trigram. Both are foundations: the first builds its base inside, the fourth carries it out into a circle of people.

## Origin and history

The six places come from the I Ching, the Chinese Book of Changes, whose 64 hexagrams Human Design takes as its gates. Ra Uru Hu, who founded the system in 1987, gave each place a name and a theme of his own.

Classical commentary already gave each place its own character. The Great Appendix, in James Legge’s translation of 1882, finds “difficulty in knowing” the first line. The top one is easy: they are the beginning and the end. Richard Wilhelm’s translation of 1924 heads this line “at the beginning”.

In the first hexagram, The Creative, Wilhelm’s first line reads “Hidden dragon. Do not act.” The strength is there, still below the surface. Human Design’s Investigator is close to that picture: a beginning that gathers itself before it shows.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune reads the line from the ecliptic longitude of each planet and each Node, at birth and at the Design moment. Its wheel starts with gate 41 at 302°, or 2° Aquarius. Each gate spans 5.625°, each line 0.9375°, or 0°56′15″.

Ulune subtracts 302° from the longitude and wraps the result into 0–360°. Divided by 5.625° and rounded down, it gives the gate’s place in the wheel. The remainder, divided by 0.9375° and rounded down, plus one, gives the line.

Line 1 is therefore the first 0.9375° of every gate, counted in the direction of the zodiac. In gate 41 it runs from 2°00′00″ to 2°56′15″ Aquarius. A longitude exactly on a boundary starts the next line.

The finer count used for the [[hd-variable|arrows]] always agrees with this line. The Earth, exactly 192 lines from the Sun, always shares its line. Without a birth time, the chart is cast at noon and every hour of the day. A line that changes in that span is marked ~.
