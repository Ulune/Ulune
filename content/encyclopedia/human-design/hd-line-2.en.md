## Meaning

Line 2 is the second place from the bottom of the hexagram, the middle of the lower trigram. Human Design calls it the Hermit. Its theme is natural talent: a gift that is simply there, often unexamined, and best tended away from other people’s eyes.

Where the second line is active, the tradition describes someone who likes to be left alone with what they do. The talent does not need to be studied the way [[hd-line-1|line 1]] studies. It grows by being practised quietly, for its own pleasure.

Others, however, tend to notice it. The Hermit is often called out of its retreat by someone who sees the gift more clearly than its owner. Ulune’s texts put it simply: line 2 is often drawn out by others who spot the talent.

So the line lives between two needs: time alone, and the right invitation. Human Design reads the wish to withdraw as healthy, not as shyness to overcome.

## In practice

- **In a gate.** A planet on the second line gives the [[hd-gate|gate]]’s theme an easy, untaught quality. The gift is lived more than explained, and may surprise its owner when someone names it. It is written after the gate number: 34.2 is gate 34, line 2.
- **In a profile.** When the Personality Sun or the Design Sun stands on line 2, it becomes half of the [[hd-profile|profile]]. Four profiles hold it: [[hd-profile-2-4|2/4]], [[hd-profile-2-5|2/5]], [[hd-profile-5-2|5/2]] and [[hd-profile-6-2|6/2]].
- **Conscious or not.** As the first number, the wish for retreat is felt and known. As the second number (5/2, 6/2), the talent works in the [[hd-layers|Design]], and others may see it long before its owner does.
- **An everyday picture.** Ulune’s own example: someone who sings only at home until a friend overhears and invites them to perform.
- **The harmonic.** Line 2 pairs with [[hd-line-5|line 5]], the middle of the upper trigram. Both are seen by others: the second is called out for its natural gift, the fifth is looked to for solutions. The 2/5 and 5/2 profiles join the pair.

## Origin and history

The six places come from the I Ching, the Chinese Book of Changes, whose 64 hexagrams Human Design takes as its gates. Ra Uru Hu, who founded the system in 1987, gave each place a name and a theme of his own.

In classical reading, the second place is central. Middle places were held to be balanced and favourable. Its natural partner is the fifth place, the ruler’s, in the middle of the upper trigram. Classical commentary often pictures it as an official working in the provinces, away from the court but in touch with the ruler. The Great Appendix, in Legge’s translation of 1882, calls the second line “the object of much commendation”.

In the first hexagram, Wilhelm’s second line reads “Dragon appearing in the field.” The strength has come out of hiding and can be seen. The Hermit’s talent, noticed by others, is close to that image.

## How Ulune calculates it

Ulune reads the line from the ecliptic longitude of each planet and each Node, at birth and at the Design moment. Its wheel starts with gate 41 at 302°, or 2° Aquarius. Each gate spans 5.625°, each line 0.9375°, or 0°56′15″.

Ulune subtracts 302° from the longitude and wraps the result into 0–360°. Divided by 5.625° and rounded down, it gives the gate’s place in the wheel. The remainder, divided by 0.9375° and rounded down, plus one, gives the line.

Line 2 is therefore the second 0.9375° of every gate, from 0.9375° to 1.875° into it, counted in the direction of the zodiac. In gate 41 it runs from 2°56′15″ to 3°52′30″ Aquarius. A longitude exactly on a boundary starts the next line.

The finer count used for the [[hd-variable|arrows]] always agrees with this line. The Earth, exactly 192 lines from the Sun, always shares its line. Without a birth time, the chart is cast at noon and every hour of the day. A line that changes in that span is marked ~.
